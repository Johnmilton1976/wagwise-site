Dog Ear Infection Symptoms — And What Causes Them

Ear infections are one of the most common reasons dogs visit a vet. The signs are recognisable — but a head tilt or loss of balance is a different, more serious problem.

The signs, ranked by how often they appear

SignWhat it suggests
Head shaking, or scratching at one earThe most common first sign of outer ear inflammation
An unpleasant smell from the earYeast or bacterial overgrowth
Redness and swelling of the ear flapInflammation; the flap itself may be involved
Discharge — brown, yellow, or blackColour helps narrow the cause (see below)
Whining when the ear is touchedPain — the ear canal is inflamed
Rubbing the side of the head along furnitureItching, often allergic
Head tiltMiddle or inner ear, or a vestibular problem — more serious
Loss of balance, circling, fallingInner ear or vestibular disease — see a vet promptly
Drooping eyelid or lip on one sideFacial nerve involvement — middle ear disease

The bottom three rows are the ones that change the urgency. A dog with a smelly ear has an outer ear infection. A dog with a head tilt has something deeper, and the difference is not cosmetic.

What the discharge colour tells you

DischargeMost likely cause
Dark brown or black, crumbly, like coffee groundsEar mites — especially in a young dog, and intensely itchy
Brown, waxy, with a sweet or musty smellYeast (Malassezia) overgrowth — very common
Yellow or greenish pusBacterial infection
Reddish-brown, wetMixed infection, or chronic change to the canal
Clear fluid, or none at all but the ear is painfulEarly inflammation, foreign body, or allergy

None of this replaces a swab. Yeast and bacteria look nothing alike under a microscope and are treated with completely different drugs — guessing wastes weeks and lets the canal thicken, which makes the next infection harder to treat.

Outer, middle and inner: why the distinction matters

Otitis externa — the outer canal. By far the most common. Itchy, smelly, uncomfortable but not dangerous. Most cases resolve with the right cleaning and the right medication.

Otitis media — the middle ear, behind the eardrum. Usually an extension of an untreated outer infection, sometimes a polyp or a grass seed. Signs include head tilt, pain when the mouth is opened, and sometimes facial nerve weakness on that side. It needs longer treatment and often imaging.

Otitis interna — the inner ear, which contains the balance organ. Signs are head tilt, nystagmus (the eyes flickering side to side), circling, falling, and sometimes vomiting. This is a neurological problem, not a hygiene problem.

A head tilt that appears suddenly, with eyes flickering or the dog unable to walk straight, is a vet visit today. It may be an inner ear infection, but it can also be vestibular disease, a stroke-like event, or a toxin — and those look similar from the outside.

Why you should not just use leftover ear drops

This is the most important practical warning on this page.

Some ear medications contain ingredients that are safe on an intact eardrum and damaging if the eardrum is ruptured. You cannot see whether the eardrum is intact without an otoscope, and chronic or severe infections rupture it more often than owners expect. Instilling an ototoxic drug into a middle ear can cause permanent hearing loss and worsen the vestibular signs.

There is a second reason: the wrong drug for the organism. An antibacterial given for a yeast infection does nothing except select for resistant bacteria, and by the time the dog reaches the clinic the infection is established and the canal is swollen shut.

The underlying cause is the real problem

Most recurrent ear infections in dogs are not primarily ear problems. Something else keeps inflaming the canal, and until that is addressed the infections return every few months.

  • Allergy — environmental or food allergy is the single most common underlying cause of recurrent otitis in dogs. If the ears are involved along with paws, face and groin, think allergy first.
  • Anatomy — floppy ears and hair growing inside the canal trap moisture and reduce airflow. Cocker spaniels, basset hounds, poodles and schnauzers are over-represented.
  • Hypothyroidism — an underactive thyroid predisposes to ear disease, along with weight gain and lethargy. Worth testing in a dog with recurrent otitis and other compatible signs.
  • Foreign bodies — grass seeds, especially in summer. A dog that was fine yesterday and is now violently shaking its head may have a seed in the canal.
  • Swimming and bathing — moisture left in the canal is a culture medium. Drying the ears after water contact helps.
  • Polyps or tumours — in older dogs with a one-sided, non-responsive infection, the vet will look for a mass.

How to clean an ear properly

Use a dog-specific ear cleaner, not water, not vinegar, not alcohol, not hydrogen peroxide. Vinegar and alcohol sting inflamed tissue, and peroxide damages the delicate lining.

Fill, massage, let the dog shake. Squeeze cleaner into the canal, massage the base of the ear for a few seconds (you will hear it squelch), then stand back and let the dog shake it out. Wipe the visible part of the flap with cotton wool.

Do not push cotton buds down the canal. You will pack debris against the eardrum. The canal is L-shaped and you cannot see round the bend.

Do not clean a painful ear. If the dog cries when the ear is touched, cleaning at home will hurt and may make it worse. That ear needs a vet and probably pain relief first.

When it is urgent

  • Sudden head tilt, nystagmus, circling or falling.
  • Facial droop on one side, or inability to close the eye.
  • A soft, fluid-filled swelling on the ear flap — an aural haematoma, which forms when a dog shakes violently and bursts a vessel.
  • Severe pain, or the dog will not let the head be touched at all.
  • Any ear problem in a dog that is also off food and lethargic.

Frequently asked questions

What are the first signs of an ear infection in dogs?
Head shaking, scratching at one ear, an unpleasant smell, redness of the ear flap and discharge. Pain when the ear is touched is common.
When is an ear infection an emergency?
A sudden head tilt, eyes flickering side to side, loss of balance, circling, or facial droop on one side. Those suggest middle or inner ear involvement and need prompt veterinary attention.
Can I use human ear drops on my dog?
No. Some ingredients are toxic to the inner ear if the eardrum is ruptured, which you cannot see at home. Wrong-drug treatment also lets the infection establish.
Why does my dog keep getting ear infections?
Recurrence usually means an underlying cause: allergy, ear anatomy, hypothyroidism, or moisture from swimming. Until that is treated, the infections return.
How do I clean my dog's ears?
Use a dog-specific ear cleaner: fill the canal, massage the base, let the dog shake, then wipe the flap with cotton wool. Never push cotton buds into the canal.
What does brown ear discharge mean in dogs?
Dark brown crumbly discharge suggests ear mites, especially in a young intensely itchy dog. Brown waxy discharge with a musty smell more often means yeast.
Are ear infections contagious to other pets?
Bacterial and yeast infections are not contagious. Ear mites are — they spread readily between pets in the same household, so all animals need treating.
Can ear infections cause a head tilt in dogs?
Yes. A head tilt indicates middle or inner ear involvement, or a vestibular problem. It is more serious than an outer ear infection and needs a vet.
