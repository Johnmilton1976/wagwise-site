Why Is My Dog Shaking? 8 Common Reasons

Shaking can be as simple as being cold or as serious as pain or poisoning. The context — other symptoms, body temperature, behavior — tells you which.

First, name the movement correctly

"Shaking" covers at least four different things, and each points somewhere different. Watch for fifteen seconds before you do anything else.

What it looks likeWhat it isTypical cause
Fine, fast whole-body vibration; dog is aware and responsiveTremorCold, fear, pain, toxin, metabolic problem
Rhythmic shivering with hunched posture, ears backShiveringCold or anxiety — the two most common and most benign
Loss of awareness, paddling legs, jaw chomping, urinationSeizureEpilepsy, toxin, brain lesion, low blood sugar
Head bobs rhythmically up and down, body still, dog fully consciousHead tremorIdiopathic head tremor syndrome — benign in most cases

That head tremor distinction saves a lot of unnecessary panic. A dog whose head bobs while it stands normally, walks normally and responds normally is usually showing idiopathic head tremor syndrome — it stops on its own, and it is not a seizure. Video it for the vet rather than rushing in.

The benign causes, in order of frequency

Cold. Small breeds, short coats, very old and very young dogs shiver first. Obvious in winter, less obvious after a swim or in an air-conditioned room.

Anxiety and fear. Thunder, fireworks, vet visits, a new person in the house. Look for the rest of the body language: tucked tail, ears back, yawning, lip licking, refusing food.

Excitement. The full-body quiver before a walk or when you come home. It resolves in minutes and the dog looks happy, not distressed.

Pain — the cause most often missed

Dogs do not cry out for most pain. They tremble. Chronic pain that shows up as shivering includes:

  • Abdominal pain — hunched back, reluctance to lie down, looking at the flank. Pancreatitis is the classic.
  • Back or neck pain — IVDD in dachshunds and other long-backed breeds; a dog that will not turn its head or yelps when picked up.
  • Dental pain — often missed entirely; a dog that trembles while eating and chews on one side.
  • Arthritis — a senior dog trembling after a walk, or first thing in the morning.
  • Anal gland impaction or abscess — scooting plus trembling plus licking at the rear.

A useful test: if the trembling started after activity, or it stops when the dog is comfortable and warm on a soft bed, pain is high on the list.

Toxins that cause trembling

This is the group where waiting costs the dog. Trembling plus vomiting, drooling, unsteadiness or hyperactivity after possible exposure is an emergency call, not a wait-and-see.

ToxinSigns beyond trembling
Chocolate (theobromine)Vomiting, hyperactivity, racing heart, seizures
XylitolWeakness, collapse, seizures — from hypoglycaemia and liver injury
Grapes and raisinsVomiting, then kidney failure over days
Pyrethrin / permethrin flea productsTremors, drooling, twitching, unsteadiness — especially when a cat product is applied to a dog
Metaldehyde (slug pellets)Tremors, high temperature, seizures
RodenticideBleeding, weakness — tremors less typical but possible
Human medication — ADHD stimulants, antidepressants, nicotineAgitation, tremors, racing heart
The permethrin one catches people out every year. Some dog flea products and cat flea products look identical, and a concentrated permethrin spot-on meant for a dog can cause severe, sometimes fatal, tremors in a cat — and an accidental high dose in a small dog causes the same picture. Never swap products between species.

Metabolic causes

Low blood sugar. Typical in toy breeds and puppies: trembling, weakness, wobbly walking, sometimes collapse. A small dog that trembles before breakfast is the classic presentation. A drop of honey or glucose syrup on the gums buys minutes while you call the vet.

Fever. A dog with an infection shivers to raise its temperature — the shivering is the mechanism, not the disease. A thermometer is the only way to tell this from cold.

Addison's disease. Underdiagnosed and dangerous. Waxing and waning weakness, vomiting, trembling, and collapse, often triggered by stress. An Addisonian crisis is a genuine emergency that looks like "a dog that keeps going off its legs".

Electrolyte disturbance — low calcium or low potassium, from illness or from some medications — produces tremors and muscle twitching.

Tremor syndromes specific to dogs

Idiopathic head tremor. Rhythmic vertical or horizontal head bobbing, dog fully conscious and otherwise normal, episodes of minutes. Most common in small breeds and in English bulldogs and Dobermanns. Benign in most cases; video it and show the vet.

"White shaker dog" syndrome. Generalised tremor in small white breeds — Maltese, West Highland whites, Spitz types — typically young adults. It is a steroid-responsive inflammatory condition and generally improves well with treatment. It looks alarming and is one of the more treatable causes.

Senile tremor and cognitive change. Older dogs can develop mild tremor alongside the other signs of cognitive dysfunction — pacing, staring at walls, reversed day and night. Worth a vet visit, and worth asking about cognitive support.

The 60-second assessment

Is the dog aware and responsive? If yes, it is a tremor, not a seizure.

Take the temperature. Around 38.0–39.2 °C is normal. High means fever or a toxin; low means shock or cold. Do not judge by the ears.

Check the gums. Pink and wet is normal. Pale, grey or brick red is a reason to go now.

Look at the pupils. Equal and responsive is reassuring. Pinpoint or widely dilated and unresponsive is not.

Watch for a trigger. Did this start after a walk, a meal, a flea treatment, a thunderstorm, or when you came home? The answer usually names the cause.

What to do, by scenario

Cold or anxious, otherwise normal: warm the dog, reduce the stressor, and observe. If it resolves within minutes and the dog behaves normally, nothing more is needed.

Trembling plus pain signs: vet visit today. Do not give human painkillers — ibuprofen and paracetamol are toxic to dogs, and the wrong painkiller can worsen an abdominal problem.

Trembling plus vomiting, drooling or unsteadiness: emergency. Bring the packaging of anything the dog may have eaten.

Full seizure — loss of awareness, paddling: do not restrain the dog or put anything in its mouth. Clear the area, time the episode, keep it cool, and call the vet. A seizure lasting over five minutes, or more than two in 24 hours, is an emergency.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my dog shaking but acting normal?
Excitement, mild cold, or stress can cause shivering with normal behaviour. If it passes quickly and the dog is otherwise well, it is usually nothing.
Why is my dog trembling for no reason?
There is always a reason — cold, pain, nausea, low blood sugar, fever or a toxin. If the trembling is persistent and you cannot identify a trigger, get the dog examined.
Is head bobbing a seizure in dogs?
Usually not. Idiopathic head tremor looks like rhythmic head bobbing while the dog stays fully conscious and otherwise normal. Video it for the vet.
Can flea treatment make a dog shake?
Yes. Permethrin and pyrethrin products cause tremors, drooling and unsteadiness, especially at high doses or in small dogs. Never use a cat product on a dog.
Why does my small dog shake in the morning?
Possible low blood sugar. Toy breeds and puppies have small glycogen reserves — trembling before breakfast is the classic sign. Feed more frequent small meals and have it checked.
When is dog shaking an emergency?
Trembling with vomiting, drooling or unsteadiness after possible toxin exposure, a seizure lasting over five minutes, collapse, pale gums, or trembling with a distended abdomen.
Do dogs shake when they are in pain?
Yes — trembling is one of the main ways dogs show pain, especially abdominal, back, dental and joint pain. Dogs rarely cry out.
Why does my old dog shake?
Arthritis, dental pain, muscle weakness, cognitive dysfunction, or a metabolic problem. In a senior dog, persistent trembling should always be examined rather than put down to age.
