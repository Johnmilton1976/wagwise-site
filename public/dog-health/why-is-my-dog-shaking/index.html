Why Is My Dog Shaking? 8 Common Reasons

Shaking can be as simple as being cold or as serious as pain or poisoning. The context — other symptoms, body temperature, behavior — tells you which.

Harmless causes

  • Cold — small and short-haired dogs shiver to warm up.
  • Excitement or anticipation (the zoomie shake before a walk).
  • Stress or anxiety, including thunderstorms.

Medical causes to know

Pain, fever, nausea, low blood sugar (common in tiny breeds), and toxins like chocolate or xylitol can all cause trembling. So can old-age weakness.

Sudden shaking with weakness, collapse, vomiting, or known toxin exposure is an emergency — call a vet now.

How to assess

  • Is your dog warm and calm? Probably benign.
  • Shaking plus lethargy, panting, or hiding? Likely pain or illness.
  • Shaking plus a known toxin? Treat as urgent.

What to do

Warm a cold dog, soothe an anxious one, and watch. If shaking persists without an obvious cause or comes with other symptoms, a vet visit is the safe call.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my dog shaking but acting normal?
Excitement, mild cold, or stress can cause shivering with normal behavior. If it passes quickly, it is usually nothing.
When is dog shaking an emergency?
Shaking with weakness, collapse, vomiting, or possible toxin ingestion needs immediate veterinary care.
Why does my small dog shake so much?
Tiny breeds lose body heat fast and are prone to low blood sugar shakes. A sweater and regular meals often help.
