Dog Vomiting Causes — What's Normal and What's Urgent

A dog that vomits once, then acts normal, is usually fine. Vomiting paired with lethargy, repeated episodes, or a swollen belly is a different story.

First, decide: is this vomiting or regurgitation?

These are different events and they point at different problems. Getting the label right changes what you should do tonight.

VomitingRegurgitation
EffortActive — retching, abdominal heaving, drooling firstPassive — food simply appears, often with no warning
TimingVariableUsually within 30 minutes of eating
ContentDigested or partly digested, bile-stained, may be liquidUndigested food, still recognisable, often tube-shaped
Usual causeStomach or intestinal disease, toxin, systemic illnessEating too fast, oesophageal problem, megaoesophagus

Regurgitation after a fast meal is usually managed with a slow feeder and smaller, more frequent meals. Regurgitation that happens repeatedly, or with weight loss, is an oesophageal problem and needs a vet.

Read the vomit before you clean it up

It is unpleasant advice, but the content is diagnostic information your vet will ask about — and photographing it on a phone is the fastest way to answer those questions later.

A swallowed linear object — string, tinsel, a sock, a length of rope — is not a "wait and see". It can saw through the intestine as it moves. Never pull it if you see it at the mouth.

The frequency rule

The single most useful triage question is not what came up, but how many times and how the dog looks between episodes.

  • One vomit, then normal energy, normal appetite, normal drinking — usually nothing. Watch for 12 hours.
  • Two or three vomits in a day, dog still bright — withhold food for 4–6 hours, then small bland meals. Vet if it continues past 24 hours.
  • Vomiting more than 3–4 times in a day, or any vomiting with lethargy — vet visit today.
  • Vomiting with a distended, hard, painful belly, or unproductive retching — emergency, now. See the GDV section.

The emergencies that hide inside "just vomiting"

Four conditions present as vomiting and kill dogs that wait.

GDV — gastric dilatation-volvulus. The stomach fills with gas and twists. Signs: a hard, swollen belly that sounds like a drum, unproductive retching (trying to vomit and bringing up nothing or only foam), restlessness, drooling, rapid deterioration. This is a surgical emergency with a mortality rate that climbs steeply by the hour — a dog with these signs should be in a car, not on a forum.

Foreign body obstruction. Vomiting that persists, often after eating or drinking, with a dog that wants to eat but cannot keep anything down. Small dogs and puppies swallow stones, corn cobs, toys and bones.

Toxin ingestion. Chocolate, grapes and raisins, xylitol, onions, antifreeze, rodenticide, and human medication. See the toxic foods list. Do not wait for vomiting to start — call a vet or a poison service with the amount and the dog's weight.

Pancreatitis. Vomiting plus a hunched posture, abdominal pain, and often a history of a fatty meal. A dog with a pancreatitis history is at risk every time someone leaves a plate within reach.

What to do at home (the 12-hour protocol)

This applies only to an adult dog that vomited once or twice, is bright, and has no red-flag signs.

Hour 0–4: withhold food. Do not withhold water — offer small amounts frequently rather than a full bowl, so a dog that is going to vomit again does not take in a large volume first.

Hour 4–6: offer a small bland meal — plain boiled chicken with white rice, or plain boiled rice with a little cottage cheese. A couple of tablespoons, not a bowl.

Hour 6–24: if it stays down, two or three more small meals. If it comes back up, stop and call the vet — continuing to feed a dog that cannot keep food down only adds to the risk of aspiration.

Never give human anti-nausea medication or painkillers without veterinary instruction. Ibuprofen and paracetamol are toxic to dogs, and metoclopramide dosing is weight-dependent and contraindicated in obstruction — the very case you cannot rule out at home.

Checking for dehydration

Vomiting dehydrates dogs faster than most owners expect, and puppies and small breeds worse than large ones. Two checks you can do at home:

Skin tent. Gently lift the skin over the shoulders and release. It should snap back immediately. If it stays up or returns slowly, the dog is dehydrated.

Gums. They should be pink and wet. Pale, dry or tacky gums are a warning sign. Press a finger on the gum until it blanches white and count how long it takes to turn pink again — over two seconds is slow.

Causes worth knowing beyond "ate something odd"

Chronic low-grade vomiting — a dog that vomits bile every few mornings, or intermittently for weeks — often has bilious vomiting syndrome, inflammatory bowel disease, or a food intolerance. This is not an emergency but it is not normal either, and it deserves a proper work-up rather than repeated bland diets.

Vomiting in an unvaccinated or incompletely vaccinated dog — parovirus is a real possibility and is a fast-moving emergency, particularly in puppies. Bloody diarrhoea plus vomiting plus lethargy is the classic picture.

Vomiting with increased drinking and urination — points toward kidney disease, diabetes, or an endocrine problem like Addison's disease. Addisonian crisis can present as vomiting, weakness and collapse and is easy to miss.

Prevention that actually works

Slow feeders for dogs that inhale meals — a large part of daily vomiting in healthy dogs is simply eating too fast and drinking immediately afterwards.

Split the meals. Two or three smaller meals instead of one large one reduces bilious vomiting and bloat risk.

Bin discipline. Most vomiting episodes that reach a clinic start with a bin, a compost heap, or a plate left on a coffee table.

No sudden food changes. Transition over 7–10 days, mixing increasing proportions of the new food.

Frequently asked questions

Should I feed my dog after vomiting?
Withhold food for 4–6 hours, keep offering small amounts of water, then give a small bland meal such as boiled chicken and rice. If it stays down, continue small meals over 24 hours.
When should I worry about my dog vomiting?
Three or more episodes in a day, vomiting with lethargy, a swollen or painful belly, unproductive retching, blood, or any suspected toxin exposure. Those need a vet now.
What does yellow vomit mean in dogs?
Usually bile from an empty stomach, common in dogs fed once a day. Splitting meals often fixes it. Recurrent bile vomiting should still be checked.
What does brown grainy vomit mean?
It looks like coffee grounds and indicates partially digested blood from stomach bleeding. That is an emergency.
What is the difference between vomiting and regurgitation?
Vomiting is active, with retching and drooling. Regurgitation is passive — undigested food appears soon after eating with no effort, often from eating too fast.
Is vomiting a sign of parovirus?
It can be, especially with bloody diarrhoea and lethargy in a puppy or an unvaccinated dog. That combination is an emergency.
Why does my dog vomit every morning?
Bilious vomiting syndrome — an empty stomach producing bile — is the most common cause. Splitting meals and a late-night snack usually help. Persistent cases need a work-up.
Can I give my dog human anti-nausea medicine?
No, not without veterinary instruction. Several human medications are toxic to dogs, and anti-nausea drugs are contraindicated in obstruction, which you cannot rule out at home.
