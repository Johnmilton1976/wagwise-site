Dog Not Eating but Drinking Water — Should You Worry?

A dog that drinks but refuses food for a day is often just under the weather. Skipping food for more than two days, or any lethargy with it, needs a vet.

The pattern matters more than the skipped meal

"Not eating but drinking" is not one condition. It is a pattern with a shortlist of causes, and the combination of normal thirst with no appetite is more informative than either sign alone.

The distinction that matters clinically: a dog that refuses food but still drinks is usually not dehydrated yet, which buys time. A dog that refuses both is a faster problem. And a dog that drinks much more than usual while not eating is pointing at a specific set of diseases rather than a mild upset.

How long a dog can actually go without food

Dogs cope with short fasts far better than people assume, and far better than cats do. Cats mobilise fat in a way that can destroy the liver within days; dogs are much more resistant to that. This is why a one-day fast in a healthy adult dog is not itself dangerous.

DogWhen refusal becomes a vet visitWhy
Puppy under 12 weeks12–24 hours, or two skipped mealsNo reserves, dehydrates fast, and parvovirus is a real risk
Toy / small breed adult24 hoursLow glycogen reserves; hypoglycaemia risk in the smallest
Medium / large adult48 hoursGenerally tolerant of a fast, but not of an undiagnosed cause
Senior or chronically ill24 hoursLess reserve, and weight loss is harder to recover from
Any dog refusing water tooImmediatelyDehydration becomes the primary danger, not nutrition

Those thresholds are about hydration and energy. The reason to see a vet is almost never the fasting itself — it is that the cause behind it might be something that gets worse every day.

What "drinking more than usual" points to

A dog that stops eating and starts drinking noticeably more is displaying a recognised pattern — polyuria and polydipsia with anorexia. The differential list is short and worth knowing, because it changes how urgently you act.

ConditionOther signs to look for
Kidney diseaseIncreased urination, dilute urine, bad breath, weight loss, vomiting
Diabetes mellitusLarge volumes of urine, weight loss despite eating previously well, sudden cataracts in older dogs
PancreatitisHunched back, abdominal pain, vomiting, recent fatty meal
Pyometra (unspayed female)Drinking a lot, lethargy, discharge, recent season 2–8 weeks ago — surgical emergency
Addison's diseaseWaxing and waning lethargy, vomiting, weakness — can present as a crisis
Fever / infectionWarm ears and paws, shivering, low energy
Dental or oral painDrooling, dropping food, chewing on one side, bad breath
NauseaLip licking, swallowing repeatedly, drooling, eating grass

Two of those are surgical emergencies that hide behind "off his food": pyometra in an unspayed female, and a foreign body obstruction in any dog that also vomits.

Four checks you can do at home

1. The mouth. Lift the lip and look at the gums — should be pink and wet, not pale, not brick red. Look for tartar build-up, a broken tooth, a swollen gum, or something stuck across the roof of the mouth. Dental pain is one of the most common and most overlooked reasons a dog stops eating while still drinking normally.

2. Hydration. Lift the skin over the shoulders. It should snap back instantly. Slow return means dehydration, which changes the timeline to "today".

3. Hydration status of the gums. Press a finger on the gum until it goes white, then count until the colour returns. Over two seconds is slow.

4. Temperature, if you have a rectal thermometer. Normal is roughly 38.0–39.2 °C. A fever explains the loss of appetite and the thirst in one reading. Do not guess by feel — ears and noses are unreliable indicators.

Do not press hard on the abdomen to "check for pain". A dog with an obstruction, an inflamed pancreas or a pyometra can be seriously harmed by a firm hand, and the information you get is not reliable.

What actually helps a fussy or nauseous dog eat

Warm the food. Warming to body temperature releases aroma, and smell is what drives a dog's appetite. A few seconds in the microwave, stirred and tested for hot spots.

Add a small amount of something strong-smelling — a spoon of plain boiled chicken, a little low-sodium chicken broth with no onion or garlic, or a teaspoon of plain yoghurt. Mixed through, not on top, so the dog cannot pick out only the good part.

Feed by hand, one piece at a time. A nauseous dog will often take food from a hand when it will not approach a bowl, because the bowl is now associated with feeling sick.

Offer a small amount, frequently. Four small meals are far better than one large one that gets refused entirely.

Change the texture, not the food. A little warm water mixed into kibble, or the same food mashed, is less disruptive than switching brands.

What not to do

Do not cycle through ten different foods. Offering a new food every few hours teaches a dog to hold out for something better, and it makes a later elimination diet impossible. Pick one option and offer it consistently.

Do not force food into the mouth. A nauseous dog that is force-fed is likely to aspirate, and it damages trust at the moment you need cooperation.

Do not use human appetite stimulants or anti-nausea drugs without veterinary advice. Dosing is weight-dependent, and some are contraindicated in obstruction.

Do not assume it will pass because the dog is drinking. Drinking protects against dehydration; it does nothing about the underlying cause.

Keeping a record your vet will thank you for

Before you call, write down: how many days of reduced or zero intake; approximate water consumed per day; whether urination has increased; any vomiting or diarrhoea and how often; whether the dog is drinking more than before this started; weight change if you can weigh the dog; and any medication started in the two weeks before the refusal.

That last item is frequently the answer. Many drugs — antibiotics, NSAIDs, chemotherapy agents, some cardiac medications — cause appetite loss, and the timing is the clue.

When it is an emergency

Go now, not tomorrow, if any of these are present:

  • Refusing water as well as food.
  • An unspayed female with a recent season who is drinking a lot and lethargic — possible pyometra.
  • Vomiting plus a distended or painful abdomen.
  • Collapse, weakness, or inability to stand.
  • Pale or white gums.
  • A puppy that has skipped two meals, or any dog with bloody diarrhoea.
  • Known access to a toxin — chocolate, grapes, raisins, xylitol, rodenticide, human medication.

Frequently asked questions

How long can a dog go without eating?
A healthy adult can miss a day or two, but more than 48 hours — or any refusal in a puppy — warrants a vet visit. Refusing water as well is urgent at any age.
Why is my dog drinking a lot of water but not eating?
That specific pattern points to kidney disease, diabetes, pancreatitis, pyometra in an unspayed female, fever or an infection. It needs a vet rather than home monitoring.
Is it normal for a dog to skip a meal?
An occasional skipped meal in an otherwise bright, drinking adult dog is common and usually harmless. Repeated skipping, or skipping plus lethargy, is not.
What can I do to get my dog to eat?
Warm the food, add a small amount of strong-smelling topper mixed through, offer small amounts frequently, and try hand-feeding. Do not cycle through many different foods.
Should I worry if my dog only eats grass and drinks water?
Grass eating alone is not a diagnosis. Combined with not eating normal food, it usually indicates nausea — check for other signs and see a vet if it lasts more than a day.
Can stress stop a dog from eating?
Yes. A move, a new pet, a boarding stay, or a change in routine commonly causes a day or two of reduced appetite. It should resolve once the stressor settles.
What if my dog is not eating after surgery or medication?
Appetite loss for 24 hours after anaesthesia is common. Beyond that, or with any medication started recently, call the vet — many drugs cause nausea and the dose may need adjusting.
When is not eating an emergency?
Refusing water too, pale gums, collapse, vomiting with a distended belly, an unspayed female with a recent season who is lethargic and thirsty, or a puppy that has skipped two meals.
