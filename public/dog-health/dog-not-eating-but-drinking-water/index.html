Dog Not Eating but Drinking Water — Should You Worry?

A dog that drinks but refuses food for a day is often just under the weather. Skipping food for more than two days, or any lethargy with it, needs a vet.

ADVERTISEMENT

Benign reasons

  • Heat, a missed walk, or a change in routine.
  • A new food they dislike.
  • Mild stomach upset that passes.

When drinking-but-not-eating is concerning

If your dog drinks a lot of water yet eats nothing for over 48 hours, or seems tired, vomits, or loses weight, underlying issues like dental pain, infection, kidney trouble, or diabetes may be involved.

A dog that stops eating entirely for more than two days — or a puppy that skips two meals — should see a vet. Puppies dehydrate fast.

What you can do

Warm the food, offer a bland meal, and check the mouth for broken teeth or sores. Track water intake; sudden gulping is a symptom worth noting.

Bottom line

One quiet day is fine. Persistent refusal, especially with thirst and low energy, is your signal to call the clinic.

Frequently asked questions

How long can a dog go without eating?
A healthy adult can miss a day or two, but more than 48 hours — or any refusal in a puppy — warrants a vet visit.
Why is my dog drinking more but not eating?
Increased thirst with appetite loss can point to infection, kidney issues, or diabetes. Note the pattern and get it checked if it lasts.
Should I force-feed my dog?
No. Forcing food stresses a sick dog. Offer bland food; if they refuse for two days, let a vet diagnose the cause.
