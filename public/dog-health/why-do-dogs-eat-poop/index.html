Why Do Dogs Eat Poop? And How to Stop It

Eating poop is normal dog behaviour with a short list of causes. Punishment makes it worse; the fixes are mostly management, diet and ruling out malabsorption.

What it is called, and how common it is

The medical term is coprophagia. It is not a sign of a disturbed dog, and it is not rare — surveys suggest a substantial minority of dogs do it at some point, with the highest rates in puppies under a year and in dogs kept in kennels or confined spaces.

Most cases are behavioural and harmless. A minority point at a digestive problem that is worth finding, and there is one scenario — a dog that eats its own stool and then gets re-infected with worms — where it turns into a cycle that is genuinely hard to break.

Why punishment is the wrong answer

This is the single most important point on this page, and it is the one most owners get wrong.

A dog that is scolded for toileting indoors does not learn "don't toilet indoors". It learns "toileting near humans is dangerous". The predictable result is a dog that toilets in a back room and then eats the evidence before you find it — which reads to the owner as a completely new and worse problem, when it is actually the old one made invisible.

The same applies to chasing a dog away from stool. Chasing is attention. For a dog that eats stool partly for the reaction, being chased is a reward. Remove the reaction and you remove the reinforcement.

Never use a punishment-based approach to coprophagia. It reliably converts an occasional habit into a secret one, and it is the reason many cases seem to "suddenly get worse".

The medical checks worth doing

If a dog is eating stool alongside any of these, book a vet visit rather than trying home remedies:

  • Weight loss or difficulty maintaining weight despite a good appetite.
  • Large volume, pale, greasy or unusually smelly stool.
  • Visible undigested food in the stool.
  • Vomiting, diarrhoea, or a pot-bellied appearance.
  • Flatulence that is markedly worse than normal.
  • Any dog that has never been wormed on a proper schedule.

The two tests that pay for themselves are a faecal flotation (parasites) and a TLI test (pancreatic exocrine insufficiency). EPI is underdiagnosed, is treatable with enzyme supplementation, and produces exactly the picture of a dog that is always hungry and eating anything.

What actually works

1. Pick up immediately. Boring, unglamorous, and more effective than every product on the market. A dog cannot eat what is not there. In a multi-dog household, clear the yard before letting dogs out.

2. Teach "leave it" properly. Practise with low-value items first, reward heavily, and generalise to stool. This is the only intervention that works at a distance and in real time.

3. Manage the environment. Leash walks in areas with heavy stool, muzzle for a determined eater in public parks, and separate the dogs at toilet time if one is a scavenger.

4. Review the diet. A highly digestible diet produces less appealing stool and less undigested residue. If the current food is a low-quality one with a lot of filler, a change is worth trying for six weeks.

5. Split the meals. Two meals instead of one reduces the hunger drive that makes stool look like food.

6. Increase enrichment. For kennelled or under-exercised dogs, the behaviour usually drops when the dog has something else to do. Sniffing games, scatter feeding, chew items.

The products, honestly

The market for stool-eating deterrents is large and the evidence is thin. The two approaches that exist:

Stool additives — usually MSG, yucca, or enzymes, given to the dog so the stool tastes unpleasant. Anecdotally helpful for some dogs; controlled evidence is weak. Worth a try if the dog eats stool from other dogs and you can treat all of them.

Taste deterrents applied to stool — such as hot sauce or commercial sprays. Impractical outdoors, and it does not address the motivation.

Popular home remedies — pineapple, pumpkin, spinach — have no meaningful evidence behind them. They are harmless, so trying them costs nothing, but do not expect much and do not let them delay the medical checks.

Why cat stool is a different problem

A dog that raids the litter tray is a step up in risk. Cat faeces can carry Toxoplasma gondii, and dogs that eat cat stool are exposed to a parasite that dogs do not normally encounter. Cat litter should be physically inaccessible — a covered tray, a baby gate the cat can jump, or a tray positioned where the dog cannot reach.

There is also a practical risk: some cat medications and dewormers are excreted in faeces, and a dog eating that stool gets a dose of a drug dosed for a cat.

When to see a vet

Urgently if the dog is eating stool alongside weight loss, vomiting, or diarrhoea — that combination points at malabsorption or EPI rather than habit.

Routinely if the behaviour is persistent beyond 12 months of age, if it started suddenly in an adult dog, or if there has never been a faecal test.

Not urgently for a healthy, well-conditioned dog that occasionally eats stool. Manage it, do not panic about it, and do not punish it.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my dog eat poop?
Usually habit or exploration, especially in puppies. Medical causes worth ruling out include parasites, malabsorption and pancreatic exocrine insufficiency, which produce undigested food in the stool.
Is eating poop harmful to dogs?
Usually not directly, but it re-exposes the dog to parasites and can transmit bacteria. It also creates a worm reinfection cycle if the dog is not on a proper worming schedule.
How do I stop my dog eating poop?
Pick it up immediately, teach a solid 'leave it', review the diet, split meals, and increase enrichment. Punishment makes it worse — it teaches the dog to do it out of sight.
Do pineapple or pumpkin stop dogs eating poop?
There is no meaningful evidence behind either. They are harmless to try, but do not rely on them or let them delay a vet check if other signs are present.
Why did my dog suddenly start eating poop?
In an adult dog, a sudden onset warrants a vet visit — parasites, malabsorption and pancreatic problems are possibilities. Also check for a recent change in food, routine or household stress.
Should I punish my dog for eating poop?
No. Punishment teaches the dog to toilet and eat it out of sight, and it damages trust. It is the single most common reason the behaviour appears to get worse.
Why does my dog eat cat poop?
Cat faeces is high in protein and dogs find it appealing. The risk is Toxoplasma exposure and medication excreted in cat stool — keep the litter tray physically inaccessible.
Can worms cause a dog to eat poop?
A heavy worm burden can increase appetite and reduce nutrient absorption, which can drive the behaviour. A faecal test and correct worming is part of the work-up.
