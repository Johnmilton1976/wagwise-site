Editorial & Medical Review Standards

WagWise Pet is an independent pet care resource written for everyday dog and cat owners. Because most of what we publish touches health or behaviour, we hold it to a clear standard. This page explains exactly how a guide is built, who stands behind it, and what we will not do.

Who writes and reviews WagWise

Guides are researched and written by the WagWise editorial team — a group of writers who specialise in reading and summarising veterinary and training literature in plain language. We do not invent individual authors or reviewers. No article on this site claims to be written or reviewed by a named veterinarian unless a real, verifiable veterinarian did so. Where a topic is medical, we point you to a licensed vet rather than pretending to be one. Our authority comes from the sources we cite, not from a borrowed white coat.

Expert citations, not borrowed authority

Because our topics are medical, we cite the named veterinary experts and specialist teams behind the guidance we summarise — for example the Veterinary Toxicology team at Pet Poison Helpline, or the veterinarians at the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center. These appear in two places on every relevant article:

  • The Sources block lists the public references, each linked so you can read them yourself.
  • The Expert sources block names the veterinary experts and teams whose published work underpins the article.

This is honest attribution: we are summarising experts' published guidance, and we say so by name. It is not the same as claiming those experts reviewed or approved this specific article — we never do that unless they actually did. The distinction matters: a fabricated "reviewed by Dr. X" line would be deceptive and would be penalised by search engines, whereas transparent citation of real experts strengthens trust without lying.

How we research a guide

Every health or behaviour claim is checked against named, public reference organisations rather than against other blogs. The core sources we draw on include:

  • The American Kennel Club (AKC) — expert advice library
  • The American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA)
  • The ASPCA — animal poison control and behaviour guidance
  • The Merck Veterinary Manual
  • Cornell Feline Health Center
  • International Cat Care
  • The American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior (AVSAB)

You can see the exact sources for any guide at the bottom of that article, with links out, so you can check us rather than trust us.

The check-date system

Every guide carries a "checked" date under its Sources block. That date is the last time the facts were compared against those references — not the day the page was first published or reformatted. When a source updates (a fee change, a revised guideline, new research), we revisit the guide and move the check date forward. Substantive corrections are reflected in that date too.

We separate urgent from optional

Where a symptom needs same-day veterinary care, the guide says so at the point where it matters — not in a vague warning at the end. We would rather tell you to call a vet than risk you waiting on a blog post.

No fear-mongering, no guarantees

We do not promise outcomes, and we do not stretch a study's finding further than the source does. Pet health content earns trust by being calm and accurate, not by being alarming.

Corrections policy

If something here is wrong, out of date, or contradicts the source we cited, tell us at contact@wagwise.org with the page address and the source that disagrees. We fix it, move the check date, and note the change. We would rather be correct than defensive.

How we make money

Some product pages contain affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you. Our picks are based on usefulness, never on commission, and we label affiliate links as such.

WagWise is not a veterinary practice

This site is general information, not veterinary advice, diagnosis, or treatment. For anything urgent, medical, or about your specific pet, contact a licensed veterinarian or your nearest animal clinic. In an emergency, do not wait for a website.
