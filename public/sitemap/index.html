ARTICLE

How to Crate Train a Puppy — First Night Included

Crate training works best when the crate feels like a den, not a cage. Feed meals inside, reward calm time, and build up to a full night over one to two weeks.

ARTICLE

How to Stop Puppy Biting — Without Yelling

Puppy biting is not aggression — it is teething and play. The fix is to redirect to a toy, reward gentle mouths, and make a brief withdrawal when teeth touch skin.

ARTICLE

How to Leash Train a Dog That Pulls

Dogs pull because pulling works — it gets them where they want to go. The fix is simple: the walk only continues when the leash is slack.

ARTICLE

Dog Vomiting Causes — What's Normal and What's Urgent

A dog that vomits once, then acts normal, is usually fine. Vomiting paired with lethargy, repeated episodes, or a swollen belly is a different story.

ARTICLE

Dog Ear Infection Symptoms and Home Care

The classic signs are head shaking, scratching at the ear, a sour smell, and red or waxy canals. Most infections need prescription drops, not just cleaning.

ARTICLE

Dog Not Eating but Drinking Water — Should You Worry?

A dog that drinks but refuses food for a day is often just under the weather. Skipping food for more than two days, or any lethargy with it, needs a vet.

ARTICLE

Why Is My Dog Shaking? 8 Common Reasons

Shaking can be as simple as being cold or as serious as pain or poisoning. The context — other symptoms, body temperature, behavior — tells you which.

ARTICLE

Why Do Dogs Eat Grass? (And When to Stop Them)

Most dogs eat grass now and then and are perfectly fine. It is often boredom or instinct — not a sign your dog is sick.

ARTICLE

Why Do Dogs Eat Poop (Coprophagia) and How to Stop It

Eating poop is gross but common, especially in puppies. It is usually behavioral — boredom, exploration, or copying the mother — and rarely a medical emergency.

ARTICLE

Foods Toxic to Dogs — The List Every Owner Needs

The most dangerous are chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions and garlic, xylitol (in sugar-free gum), and alcohol. Even small amounts can be serious for a small dog.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Bananas? Yes — in Moderation

Yes, bananas are safe for dogs in small amounts. They are a healthy treat, but their sugar and fiber mean they should stay an occasional snack, not a daily staple.

ARTICLE

How Much Should I Feed My Dog? (By Weight and Age)

Start from your dog's target weight and the food's calorie label, then adjust by body condition — you should feel ribs but not see them.

ARTICLE

How to Trim Dog Nails Without the Struggle

Trim small amounts frequently and you avoid the quick — the sensitive vein inside the nail. A scratchboard or grinder also works if clippers scare your dog.

ARTICLE

How to Brush Your Dog's Teeth (And Why It Matters)

Most adult dogs have some gum disease by age three. Two minutes of brushing most days prevents the pain, tooth loss, and cost that follow.

ARTICLE

Cat Litter Training — Faster Than You Think

Cats are naturally clean and most use a litter box with almost no teaching. The work is in the setup: the right box, the right litter, and a good location.

ARTICLE

How to Stop a Cat Scratching Furniture

Scratching is normal cat behavior you cannot train away — but you can redirect it. Give appealing posts and make the furniture boring.

ARTICLE

How to Stop a Cat Spraying Indoors

Spraying is your cat marking territory with urine on vertical surfaces — different from litter accidents. The fix is reducing stress and conflict, not punishment.

ARTICLE

Best Dog Crate 2026 — Practical Picks, Not Hype

The best crate is the one your dog accepts and you can clean easily. We prefer sturdy wire crates with a divider for puppies and a secure plastic crate for travel.

ARTICLE

Best Dog Harness for Pulling — Front-Clip Picks

A front-clip harness turns a pulling dog back toward you, which gently discourages leaning in. We favor padded, adjustable, easy-on designs over trendy ones.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Apples? Yes — Seeds and Core Out

Yes, apples are safe and healthy for dogs in moderation. The flesh is fine; the seeds and core are the parts to skip.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Watermelon? Yes — Rind and Seeds Off

Yes, watermelon is safe for dogs — the red flesh only. Leave out the rind and the seeds, which can upset the stomach or cause a blockage.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Cheese? In Small Amounts, Sometimes

Most dogs can eat a small amount of cheese, but many are lactose intolerant and all should keep it occasional because of the fat and salt.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Peanut Butter? Yes — Read the Label First

Yes, plain peanut butter is a great dog treat — but only if it contains no xylitol, which is extremely toxic to dogs. Always read the label.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Carrots? Yes — A Near-Perfect Treat

Yes, carrots are one of the best treats you can give a dog: low in calories, high in fiber and vitamin A, and a crunchy chew many dogs enjoy.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Eggs? Cooked, Plain, and Moderate

Yes, cooked eggs are a healthy, protein-rich addition to a dog's diet. Raw eggs are best avoided because of salmonella risk and a biotin concern.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Chicken? Cooked, Plain, Boneless

Yes, plain cooked chicken is one of the safest, most common proteins for dogs. The risks are cooked bones and rich seasoning, not the meat itself.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Drink Milk? Usually Not Worth It

Most dogs should skip milk. Adult dogs are commonly lactose intolerant, and a bowl of milk more often leads to gas and diarrhea than any benefit.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Strawberries? Yes — Wash and Limit

Yes, strawberries are safe for dogs in moderation. They are low in calories and full of vitamin C, but their natural sugar means they should stay an occasional treat.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Grapes? NO — They Are Toxic

No — grapes and raisins are toxic to dogs and can cause sudden, severe kidney failure. There is no safe amount, so treat any ingestion as an emergency.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Chocolate? NO — It Is Toxic

No — chocolate is toxic to dogs because it contains theobromine, which they cannot metabolize well. Dark and baking chocolate are the most dangerous.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Avocado? Risky — Best Avoided

Avocado is best avoided. The flesh is the safest part but still high in fat; the pit is a choking hazard and the skin and leaves carry persin, which upsets the gut.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Blueberries? Yes — A Tiny Antioxidant Boost

Yes — blueberries are one of the safest, healthiest treats you can give a dog. They are low in calories and packed with antioxidants, and most dogs love the burst of sweetness.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Pumpkin? Yes — Plain, Cooked, and Helpful

Yes — plain cooked pumpkin (never the sugary pie filling) is one of the most useful foods to add to a dog's bowl. Its fiber helps firm up loose stool and ease mild constipation.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Sweet Potato? Yes — Cooked, Plain

Yes — plain cooked sweet potato is one of the better carbohydrate treats you can share. It is packed with fiber and vitamin A, but only once it is cooked and freed of skin, salt, and spices.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Rice? Yes — Plain White Rice for Upset Tummies

Yes — plain cooked white rice is one of the safest, most digestible foods for dogs, and it is the backbone of the standard bland diet vets recommend for mild tummy upset.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Cucumber? Yes — a Low-Calorie Crunch

Yes — cucumber is a safe, crunchy, near-zero-calorie treat that is mostly water, so it is a great low-fat option for dogs, including those watching their weight. Peel it, and skip the pickles.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Broccoli? Yes — in Small Amounts

Yes — broccoli is safe and genuinely healthy for dogs in small amounts. The catch is that too much can irritate the stomach and, in large doses, affect the thyroid, so portion control is the whole game.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Green Beans? Yes — a Low-Cal Diet Staple

Yes — plain green beans are a safe, low-calorie, high-fiber snack dogs can eat raw or cooked. They are a common diet food for overweight dogs, as long as they stay plain.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Pineapple? Yes — in Small Pieces

Yes — fresh pineapple is a safe, vitamin-C-rich treat in small amounts. The flesh only, cut into small pieces; skip the tough core, skin, and the sugary canned version.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Mango? Yes — Peeled, Pitted, Small

Yes — fresh mango flesh is a safe, vitamin-rich treat in small pieces. Peel it, remove the pit, and skip the skin; the pit is a choking hazard and the skin is hard to digest.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Potato? Cooked Only, Never Raw

Yes — fully cooked potato is safe for dogs, but raw potato is toxic and fried or buttered potato is off the menu. Plain, boiled or baked, and never green or sprouted.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Spinach? Yes — in Small Amounts

Yes — plain spinach is safe for dogs in small amounts. It is nutrient-rich, but the natural oxalates mean it should stay an occasional treat, not a daily heap.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Tomatoes? Ripe Flesh, No Leaves

Yes — ripe red tomato flesh is safe in small amounts, but the green parts, stems, and leaves contain solanine and are toxic. Skip sauces and seasoned tomato.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Bread? Yes — Plain, but Empty Calories

Yes — a small piece of plain, fully baked bread is not toxic to dogs, but it is mostly empty calories. The one real emergency is raw dough, which can bloat the stomach and ferment into alcohol.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Popcorn? Yes — Air-Popped and Plain

Yes — plain air-popped popcorn is a light, low-calorie treat many dogs love, but buttered and salted popcorn is not, and unpopped kernels are a choking and teeth risk. Plain and fully popped is the rule.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Honey? Yes — in Tiny Amounts

Yes — a lick of plain honey is not toxic to dogs, but it is essentially sugar, so it belongs in the 'tiny treat' category. The two dogs who should skip it entirely are puppies under a year and diabetic dogs.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Yogurt? Yes — Plain and Unsweetened

Yes — plain, unsweetened yogurt is a safe, gut-friendly treat for many dogs, thanks to lower lactose than milk and live cultures. The catches are lactose intolerance and, above all, any yogurt sweetened with xylitol.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Salmon? Yes — Cooked, Never Raw

Yes — cooked salmon is one of the best fish you can offer a dog, packed with omega-3s and lean protein. The one hard rule: never feed it raw, because raw salmon can carry a parasite that is fatal to dogs.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Shrimp? Yes — Cooked and Peeled

Yes — cooked, peeled shrimp is a lean, protein-rich treat many dogs love. The rules are simple: cook it, peel it, and skip the garlic butter and frying that make shrimp dangerous.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Peas? Yes — a Healthy, Low-Fat Veggie

Yes — plain peas are a safe, low-calorie, fiber-rich vegetable that many dogs enjoy, and they show up in plenty of quality dog foods. The catches are canned peas (too much salt) and whole pods, which can choke a small dog.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Corn? Yes — the Kernels, Not the Cob

Yes — plain cooked corn kernels are safe and show up in many dog foods, but the corn cob is a serious hazard: it does not digest and can block a dog's intestine, often needing surgery. Kernels yes, cob never.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Onion? No — Even a Little Is Toxic

No — onion is toxic to dogs in every form, with no safe dose. Its sulfur compounds destroy red blood cells and cause hemolytic anemia, often days after eating. Onion powder hiding in sauces and broths is the most common source.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Garlic? No — It's Still Allium Toxicity

No — garlic is in the same Allium family as onion and causes the same red-blood-cell damage. The 'garlic for fleas' folk remedy is unsafe and unsupported, and even powdered garlic in spice rubs can harm a small dog.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Macadamia Nuts? No — They Are Toxic

No — macadamia nuts are one of the most toxic nuts for dogs. Within 12 hours of eating even a small number, dogs can develop back-leg weakness, tremors, vomiting, and fever. There is no safe amount.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Cherries? Flesh Yes — Pits Never

Cherry flesh itself is not toxic, but the pits, stems, and leaves contain cyanide compounds — and pits can also choke a dog or block the intestine. The practical answer: no cherries with pits, ever.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Peaches? Flesh Yes — Pit Is a Hazard

Fresh peach flesh is safe for dogs in small amounts, but the pit is a double hazard: it contains cyanide compounds and can choke a dog or block the intestine. Canned peaches in syrup are out entirely.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Plums? Flesh Yes — Pit Is a Hazard

Plum flesh is not toxic, but the pit carries the same cyanide and obstruction risks as peach and cherry pits — and plums are small enough that a dog often swallows the fruit whole. Prunes are a sugar trap.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Xylitol? No — It Can Be Deadly Fast

No — xylitol (birch sugar) is one of the deadliest common ingredients for dogs. A tiny amount triggers a rapid insulin surge, crash blood sugar, and possible liver failure within hours. Check every label.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Drink Alcohol? No — Even Sips Are Risky

No — dogs cannot tolerate alcohol at all. Their small body size means even a sip of a strong drink can cause vomiting, disorientation, low blood sugar, and seizures. Rises dough and fermented foods count too.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Have Caffeine? No — Coffee, Tea, Pills Are Toxic

No — caffeine in every form (coffee, tea, energy drinks, pills, grounds) is toxic to dogs. It speeds the heart and nervous system, and small dogs reach dangerous levels with just a lap or two of coffee.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Raw Dough? No — It's a Double Emergency

No — raw yeast dough keeps rising inside a dog's warm stomach, causing painful bloat and alcohol poisoning at the same time. It is one of the few kitchen hazards that is a true drop-everything emergency.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Almonds? Not Recommended — Here's Why

Almonds are not poisonous to dogs the way macadamia nuts are, but vets still say no: they are a choking hazard, hard to digest, and loaded with fat that can trigger pancreatitis. Salted or flavored almonds are worse.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Walnuts? No — Especially Black Walnuts

Walnuts are not recommended for dogs at all. English walnuts mainly pose choking and pancreatitis risk, but black walnuts can carry a mold toxin that causes tremors and seizures. Moldy walnuts of any kind are an emergency.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Ham? Small Nibbles Only — Here's Why

Ham is not poisonous, but it is one of the worst everyday meats for dogs: very salty, high in fat, and often cured with garlic or onion powder. A small plain nibble on a holiday is fine — a ham habit is not.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Ice Cream? Mostly No — Safer Options Inside

Ice cream is not toxic in itself, but between lactose upset, sugar, and flavorings like chocolate or xylitol, it is a treat to skip. A small lick of plain vanilla is low risk — a bowl is not, and some flavors are emergencies.

ARTICLE

Cat Hairballs — What's Normal and When to Worry

A hairball now and then is normal grooming housekeeping. The pattern is what matters — how often, how easily, and whether your cat is otherwise fine.

ARTICLE

Why Does My Cat Meow at Night — and How to Make It Stop

A cat yelling at your bedroom door at 3 a.m. is not being difficult. The cause is usually energy, hunger, or a habit you accidentally trained — and the fix is a routine, not a punishment.
