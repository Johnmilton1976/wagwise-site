ARTICLE

How to Crate Train a Puppy — First Night Included

Crate training works best when the crate feels like a den, not a cage. Feed meals inside, reward calm time, and build up to a full night over one to two weeks.

ARTICLE

How to Stop Puppy Biting — Without Yelling

Puppy biting is not aggression — it is teething and play. The fix is to redirect to a toy, reward gentle mouths, and make a brief withdrawal when teeth touch skin.

ARTICLE

How to Leash Train a Dog That Pulls

Dogs pull because pulling works — it gets them where they want to go. The fix is simple: the walk only continues when the leash is slack.

ARTICLE

Dog Vomiting Causes — What's Normal and What's Urgent

A dog that vomits once, then acts normal, is usually fine. Vomiting paired with lethargy, repeated episodes, or a swollen belly is a different story.

ARTICLE

Dog Ear Infection Symptoms and Home Care

The classic signs are head shaking, scratching at the ear, a sour smell, and red or waxy canals. Most infections need prescription drops, not just cleaning.

ARTICLE

Dog Not Eating but Drinking Water — Should You Worry?

A dog that drinks but refuses food for a day is often just under the weather. Skipping food for more than two days, or any lethargy with it, needs a vet.

ARTICLE

Why Is My Dog Shaking? 8 Common Reasons

Shaking can be as simple as being cold or as serious as pain or poisoning. The context — other symptoms, body temperature, behavior — tells you which.

ARTICLE

Why Do Dogs Eat Grass? (And When to Stop Them)

Most dogs eat grass now and then and are perfectly fine. It is often boredom or instinct — not a sign your dog is sick.

ARTICLE

Why Do Dogs Eat Poop (Coprophagia) and How to Stop It

Eating poop is gross but common, especially in puppies. It is usually behavioral — boredom, exploration, or copying the mother — and rarely a medical emergency.

ARTICLE

Foods Toxic to Dogs — The List Every Owner Needs

The most dangerous are chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions and garlic, xylitol (in sugar-free gum), and alcohol. Even small amounts can be serious for a small dog.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Bananas? Yes — in Moderation

Yes, bananas are safe for dogs in small amounts. They are a healthy treat, but their sugar and fiber mean they should stay an occasional snack, not a daily staple.

ARTICLE

How Much Should I Feed My Dog? (By Weight and Age)

Start from your dog's target weight and the food's calorie label, then adjust by body condition — you should feel ribs but not see them.

ARTICLE

How to Trim Dog Nails Without the Struggle

Trim small amounts frequently and you avoid the quick — the sensitive vein inside the nail. A scratchboard or grinder also works if clippers scare your dog.

ARTICLE

How to Brush Your Dog's Teeth (And Why It Matters)

Most adult dogs have some gum disease by age three. Two minutes of brushing most days prevents the pain, tooth loss, and cost that follow.

ARTICLE

Cat Litter Training — Faster Than You Think

Cats are naturally clean and most use a litter box with almost no teaching. The work is in the setup: the right box, the right litter, and a good location.

ARTICLE

How to Stop a Cat Scratching Furniture

Scratching is normal cat behavior you cannot train away — but you can redirect it. Give appealing posts and make the furniture boring.

ARTICLE

How to Stop a Cat Spraying Indoors

Spraying is your cat marking territory with urine on vertical surfaces — different from litter accidents. The fix is reducing stress and conflict, not punishment.

ARTICLE

Best Dog Crate 2026 — Practical Picks, Not Hype

The best crate is the one your dog accepts and you can clean easily. We prefer sturdy wire crates with a divider for puppies and a secure plastic crate for travel.

ARTICLE

Best Dog Harness for Pulling — Front-Clip Picks

A front-clip harness turns a pulling dog back toward you, which gently discourages leaning in. We favor padded, adjustable, easy-on designs over trendy ones.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Apples? Yes — Seeds and Core Out

Yes, apples are safe and healthy for dogs in moderation. The flesh is fine; the seeds and core are the parts to skip.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Watermelon? Yes — Rind and Seeds Off

Yes, watermelon is safe for dogs — the red flesh only. Leave out the rind and the seeds, which can upset the stomach or cause a blockage.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Cheese? In Small Amounts, Sometimes

Most dogs can eat a small amount of cheese, but many are lactose intolerant and all should keep it occasional because of the fat and salt.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Peanut Butter? Yes — Read the Label First

Yes, plain peanut butter is a great dog treat — but only if it contains no xylitol, which is extremely toxic to dogs. Always read the label.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Carrots? Yes — A Near-Perfect Treat

Yes, carrots are one of the best treats you can give a dog: low in calories, high in fiber and vitamin A, and a crunchy chew many dogs enjoy.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Eggs? Cooked, Plain, and Moderate

Yes, cooked eggs are a healthy, protein-rich addition to a dog's diet. Raw eggs are best avoided because of salmonella risk and a biotin concern.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Chicken? Cooked, Plain, Boneless

Yes, plain cooked chicken is one of the safest, most common proteins for dogs. The risks are cooked bones and rich seasoning, not the meat itself.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Drink Milk? Usually Not Worth It

Most dogs should skip milk. Adult dogs are commonly lactose intolerant, and a bowl of milk more often leads to gas and diarrhea than any benefit.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Strawberries? Yes — Wash and Limit

Yes, strawberries are safe for dogs in moderation. They are low in calories and full of vitamin C, but their natural sugar means they should stay an occasional treat.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Grapes? NO — They Are Toxic

No — grapes and raisins are toxic to dogs and can cause sudden, severe kidney failure. There is no safe amount, so treat any ingestion as an emergency.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Chocolate? NO — It Is Toxic

No — chocolate is toxic to dogs because it contains theobromine, which they cannot metabolize well. Dark and baking chocolate are the most dangerous.

ARTICLE

Can Dogs Eat Avocado? Risky — Best Avoided

Avocado is best avoided. The flesh is the safest part but still high in fat; the pit is a choking hazard and the skin and leaves carry persin, which upsets the gut.
