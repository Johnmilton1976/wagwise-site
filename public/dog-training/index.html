DOG TRAINING

Dog Training Guides — Calm, Positive Methods

Step-by-step dog and puppy training guides: crate training, leash manners, and stopping biting and pulling — all built on reward-based positive reinforcement.

Training is timing, not commands

A dog does not learn "sit" because the word is repeated. It learns because something good arrived within about three seconds of the behaviour. That window is the whole game: reward inside it and the dog connects the two; reward outside it and you reinforce whatever happened next, which is usually standing up.

This is why a marker word — "yes" — earns its place. It fires the instant the behaviour happens and buys you the two seconds it takes to get a treat out of a pocket. Mark, then pay. The dog learns the marker predicts food, and the marker becomes usable at a distance and without a hand in your pocket.

One behaviour at a time

Most training goes wrong by trying to fix three things at once. Cut every skill into its smallest version, get that reliable in a quiet room, then add one variable at a time — distance, duration, or distraction, never two together.

How long a session should be

Short and finished beats long and abandoned. A puppy has an attention span measured in minutes; five minutes twice a day teaches more than one twenty-minute session, because the last five minutes of the long session are where the mistakes and the frustration live.

End every session on a behaviour the dog already knows. That last success is what it remembers, and it is what makes the dog willing to come back out tomorrow. If a session has gone badly, do not push through to a win — stop, put the treats away, and reset later.

The five mistakes that undo progress

1. Repeating the cue. "Sit, sit, sit" teaches the dog that the first word is background noise. Say it once, wait two seconds, then make it easier.

2. Paying for the wrong moment. A dog that sits and then breaks before the treat arrives has been paid for breaking. Wait for the behaviour, mark it, then reach.

3. Training when the dog is over threshold. A dog that is already barking at another dog cannot learn. Increase distance until it can eat and think, then work there.

4. Using punishment for a fear response. Corrections suppress the visible behaviour and leave the emotion in place, which is how a dog that was once merely worried becomes a dog that snaps without warning. This is the one mistake with a genuine welfare cost.

5. Practising only in the easy place. A dog that sits perfectly in the kitchen and nowhere else has learned the kitchen. Proof the behaviour in a new location every week, at a lower difficulty than you think you need.

What "positive" actually means

Reward-based training is not permissive training. The dog still has boundaries and still hears "no" — what changes is that the boundary is taught by making the wanted behaviour worth doing, and by managing the environment so the unwanted one is not rehearsed. A puppy that cannot reach the bin does not need to be told off at the bin.

Start with the two guides most owners need first: crate training a puppy and stopping puppy biting. Both are built on the same three-second rule, applied to the two problems that make the first month hardest.

GUIDE

How to Crate Train a Puppy — First Night Included

Crate training works best when the crate feels like a den, not a cage. Feed meals inside, reward calm time, and build up to a full night over one to two weeks.

GUIDE

How to Stop Puppy Biting — Without Yelling

Puppy biting is not aggression — it is teething and play. The fix is to redirect to a toy, reward gentle mouths, and make a brief withdrawal when teeth touch skin.

GUIDE

How to Leash Train a Dog — Loose Lead in Six Weeks

A dog pulls because pulling works. The fix is not a firmer correction — it is making a loose lead the only way forward.
