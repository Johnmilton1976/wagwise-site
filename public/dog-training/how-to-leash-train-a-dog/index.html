How to Leash Train a Dog That Pulls

Dogs pull because pulling works — it gets them where they want to go. The fix is simple: the walk only continues when the leash is slack.

Why dogs pull

To a dog, forward motion is the reward. The instant it leans into the leash and moves ahead, pulling paid off. We have to make slack, not tension, the thing that earns progress.

The stop-and-go method

  • The moment the leash goes tight, stop walking. Plant your feet.
  • Wait until your dog looks back or the leash loosens, then praise and move on.
  • Repeat. A 10-minute walk may take 30 at first — that is normal and it improves fast.
Use a front-clip harness, not a choke or prong collar. Front-clip harnesses turn the dog toward you when it pulls, which is kinder and works.

Avoid the common trap

Do not keep walking while correcting — if you move forward as you pull the dog back, you have rewarded the pull. Stillness is the teacher.

Practice in low-distraction spots

Start in the living room, then the yard, then quiet streets, before busy parks. Adding distractions before the skill is solid sets everyone up to fail.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best leash for a dog that pulls?
A front-clip harness paired with a standard 4-6 foot leash. Retractable leashes teach pulling and are best avoided for training.
How long until pulling stops?
With consistent stop-and-go, most dogs walk loosely within one to two weeks. Excitable dogs take longer and need more reps.
Should I use a choke chain?
No. Choke and prong collars can damage the trachea and neck and increase fear. A front-clip harness is safer and effective.
