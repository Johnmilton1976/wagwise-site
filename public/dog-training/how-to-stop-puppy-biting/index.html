How to Stop Puppy Biting — Without Yelling

Puppy biting is not aggression — it is teething and play. The fix is to redirect to a toy, reward gentle mouths, and make a brief withdrawal when teeth touch skin.

Why is my puppy biting?

Puppy biting is not aggression — it is teething and play. Puppies explore with their mouths and bite harder than they mean to because they have not yet learned bite inhibition. Littermates teach this by yelping; you can teach it too. Understanding this is the first step to learning how to stop puppy biting without ever raising your voice.

How to stop puppy biting — the redirect method

  • When teeth touch skin, say a calm "ouch" or just go silent, and offer a toy instead.
  • If the puppy takes the toy, praise. If it keeps biting, stand up and turn away for 20-30 seconds — attention is the reward it wants.
  • Keep a toy within reach so redirection is instant, not a search mission.
Do not hit, hold the muzzle, or shout. Those can make a fearless puppy bite harder or a soft one become hand-shy.

The reverse timeout

The most reliable tool is withdrawal of attention. The instant teeth touch skin, end the interaction: stand up, step away, and stay still for 20-30 seconds. Puppies quickly learn that biting ends the fun. Consistency matters more than any single technique — everyone in the household has to do it the same way.

Extra help for teething

Cold chew toys from the freezer soothe sore gums. Rotate toys so they stay interesting. A tired puppy bites less — short training games burn energy. Offer appropriate things to chew so the puppy learns what is fair game and what is not.

What not to do

  • Do not punish with your hands — it teaches fear, not control.
  • Do not let the puppy "win" by getting more play when it bites; that rewards the behavior.
  • Do not use physical corrections like muzzle holds or taps.

When should puppy biting stop?

Biting should fade by 5-6 months as adult teeth come in and bite inhibition sets in. Soft play-mouthing may linger longer, which is normal. Persistent hard biting past that point, or any skin break with a growl, is worth a trainer's eyes.

When to get help

Most puppy biting resolves with consistency. But reach out to a qualified positive-reinforcement trainer if the biting gets harder or more frequent over time, your puppy growls or snaps from a calm state (not during play), or a bite breaks skin. Frequently asked questions

At what age do puppies stop biting?
Most puppies stop hard mouthing by 5 to 6 months, once adult teeth are in and bite inhibition is learned. Soft play-mouthing may linger longer.
How do I stop my puppy from biting me?
The reliable fix is redirection plus a reverse timeout: the instant teeth touch skin, say a calm ouch, offer a toy, and if it keeps biting, stand up and step away for 20-30 seconds. Consistency from everyone in the home is what makes it work.
Why does my puppy bite my ankles when I walk?
It is herding and chase instinct plus boredom. Stop moving, redirect to a toy, and give more exercise and mental games.
Is puppy biting a sign of aggression?
Usually no. In young puppies it is teething and play, not real aggression. The exception is a puppy that growls or snaps from a calm state, which deserves a trainer's assessment.
Should I use a bitter spray?
It can help as a backup, but the core fix is redirection and withdrawal of attention. Sprays alone rarely solve the habit.
How long does the puppy biting phase last?
Hard mouthing should fade by 5-6 months as adult teeth come in. Gentle play-mouthing may continue but should not hurt.
What if my puppy breaks skin when biting?
Clean the wound and watch it. A single playful nip that breaks skin is common, but if bites get harder, more frequent, or come with a growl from a calm state, see a qualified trainer.
Do puppies grow out of biting?
Most do, with consistent guidance. Without redirection they can learn that biting is how to get attention, so teaching bite inhibition early matters.
