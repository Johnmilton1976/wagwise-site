How to Crate Train a Puppy — Step-by-Step (First Night Included)

Crate training works best when the crate feels like a den, not a cage. Feed meals inside, reward calm time, and build up to a full night over one to two weeks.

Why crate training helps

Dogs are den animals by instinct — a small, cozy space feels safe to them, not like punishment. A crate also speeds up house training (dogs avoid soiling their bed) and gives you a safe place to put a puppy when you cannot supervise.

The goal is a dog that walks into the crate on its own. If your puppy cries the moment the door closes, you have moved too fast — back up a step.

Set up the crate right

  • Size it so your puppy can stand, turn, and lie down, but not pace. Too big and it may use a corner as a bathroom.
  • Add a soft bed and a safe chew toy. Cover the top and three sides with a light blanket to make it den-like.
  • Put it where the family is — a puppy alone in a far room will protest more.

The first week, step by step

Day 1-2: toss treats inside, let the puppy go in and out freely, no door closing. Day 3-4: close the door for 30 seconds while you stand next to it, then open. Day 5-7: build to 10-15 minutes with you in the room. Week 2: try leaving the room briefly, then a full nap, then the first night.

The first night

Bring the crate beside your bed for the first few nights. A puppy that can hear and smell you settles far faster. A potty trip at 3 a.m. is normal for an 8-10 week old — carry it out, no play, straight back in.

Common mistakes

  • Using the crate as time-out — it should mean safety, never punishment.
  • Reacting to whining by letting the puppy out every time, which trains it to cry for freedom.
  • Rushing the timeline and expecting a full night on day one.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to crate train a puppy?
Most puppies accept the crate within a week and sleep through the night within two to three weeks. An older rescue dog can take longer if it has negative associations to confine.
Should I cover the crate at night?
Covering the top and sides helps many dogs feel secure, but leave the door area open for airflow. Stop covering if your dog gets anxious or overheats.
Is it cruel to crate a dog?
No — when introduced positively and not overused. A crate should be a voluntary safe space, not a place a dog is locked for eight hours a day.
