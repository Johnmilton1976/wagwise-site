Can Dogs Eat Potato? Cooked Only, Never Raw

Yes — fully cooked potato is safe for dogs, but raw potato is toxic and fried or buttered potato is off the menu. Plain, boiled or baked, and never green or sprouted.

Is potato safe for dogs?

A plain, fully cooked potato is safe and easy to digest — it is mostly starch, with some vitamin C, vitamin B6, and potassium. The danger is in the preparation and the state of the potato: raw potatoes and green or sprouted potatoes contain solanine, a compound that is toxic to dogs, and fried or loaded potatoes add fat and salt a dog does not need.

Raw vs cooked — the solanine risk

Raw potatoes, and any potato that is green or sprouting, contain solanine, which can cause vomiting, diarrhea, confusion, and in large amounts serious poisoning. Cook thoroughly, and never feed raw potato or green/sprouted parts. The same goes for raw potato skins in quantity.

Potato nutrition at a glance

For scale, here is roughly what is in one cup of plain boiled potato (about 150 g):

NutrientAmount
Calories~135 kcal
Carbohydrate~31 g
Fiber~2.4 g
Vitamin Csome
Vitamin B6some
Potassiumgood source

A filling carb, not a nutrient powerhouse — keep portions modest.

How much potato is safe?

As an occasional plain side, a sensible portion by body weight:

Dog weightPlain cooked potato
Under 5 kg1–2 tablespoons mashed
5–15 kg1/4–1/3 cup
15–30 kg1/2 cup
30 kg and up1/2–1 cup, occasionally
Potato is calorie-dense. For a dog watching its weight, sweet potato or a low-cal veggie is often a better swap.

How to serve it

  • Boil or bake plain, with no salt, butter, oil, or seasoning.
  • Mash or cube small so a dog cannot choke.
  • Skip the skin if it is thick or you are unsure it is fully cooked.

Can puppies eat potato?

Yes, plain cooked potato in small amounts once weaned and on solid food. Never raw, green, or sprouted.

The danger: fries, butter, and green potatoes

French fries, chips, and buttered or seasoned potato are off the menu — the fat, salt, and often garlic or onion are bad for dogs, and onion and garlic are toxic. And never feed raw or green/sprouted potato (solanine). For the full list of dangerous additions, see our toxic foods guide.

When potato isn't enough

Plain potato is a small occasional carb, not a meal and not a remedy for a sick dog. Persistent vomiting or diarrhea, lethargy, or a painful belly need a veterinarian, not more food. Our vomiting guide explains what is urgent, and our feeding guide covers portion basics.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat cooked potato?
Yes — plain boiled or baked potato, with no salt, butter, or seasoning, is safe in moderation.
Can dogs eat raw potato?
No — raw potato contains solanine, which is toxic to dogs. Cook it thoroughly.
Why are green or sprouted potatoes dangerous?
They contain higher solanine levels and can poison a dog; never feed green, sprouting, or raw potato.
Can dogs eat fries or chips?
No — fries and chips are loaded with fat and salt, and often garlic or onion, which are toxic. Only plain cooked potato is safe.
How much potato can a dog eat?
Keep it occasional: a tablespoon or two for a small dog, up to a cup for a large one, plain and cooked.
Can puppies eat potato?
Yes, plain cooked potato in small amounts once weaned — never raw, green, or sprouted.
Is potato or sweet potato better for dogs?
Both are safe cooked; sweet potato is a bit more nutrient-rich, while potato is more calorie-dense. See our sweet potato guide.
What if my dog ate raw or green potato?
Call a vet or poison helpline, especially for vomiting, diarrhea, confusion, or weakness — solanine poisoning needs prompt care.
