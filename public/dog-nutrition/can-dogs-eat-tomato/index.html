Can Dogs Eat Tomatoes? Ripe Flesh, No Leaves

Yes — ripe red tomato flesh is safe in small amounts, but the green parts, stems, and leaves contain solanine and are toxic. Skip sauces and seasoned tomato.

Is tomato safe for dogs?

The ripe, red flesh of a tomato is non-toxic and can be a low-calorie treat — it brings vitamin C, potassium, and lycopene, an antioxidant. The problem is everything else on the plant: unripe (green) tomatoes and the stems, leaves, and vines contain solanine, the same toxin found in green potatoes, and that is genuinely dangerous to dogs.

Tomato nutrition at a glance

For scale, here is roughly what is in half a cup of chopped ripe tomato (about 90 g):

NutrientAmount
Calories~16 kcal
Carbohydrate~3.5 g
Fiber~1 g
Vitamin Cgood source
Potassiumsome
Lycopenepresent

Low calorie and antioxidant-rich — a light, safe bite when ripe and plain.

The danger: green tomatoes and the plant

Green (unripe) tomatoes and every green part of the plant — stems, leaves, and vines — contain solanine and are toxic to dogs. Keep dogs out of the garden and the compost of tomato plants, and never let them chew a leafy tomato stem.

How much tomato is safe?

Treats should stay under about 10% of daily calories. A sensible portion by body weight:

Dog weightSafe treat amount
Under 5 kg (toy / small)1–2 small cubes (about 1 tbsp)
5–15 kgA few cubes (2–3 tbsp)
15–30 kgUp to 1/4 cup chopped
30 kg and upUp to 1/2 cup chopped, occasionally

How to serve it

  • Use only ripe, red tomatoes — no green ones.
  • Wash, remove the stem and leaves completely, and cube small.
  • Offer plain, fresh or a little cooked — no salt, oil, or seasoning.

Can puppies eat tomato?

Yes, ripe flesh in tiny amounts once weaned and on solid food. Puppies are smaller and more sensitive, so keep it to a cube or two and watch for loose stools.

The danger: sauce, ketchup, and seasonings

Tomato sauce, ketchup, and soup are off the menu — they are loaded with salt, sugar, and often garlic or onion, which are toxic to dogs. Only plain ripe tomato flesh is safe. For the full list of dangerous additions, see our toxic foods guide.

What if my dog ate a tomato leaf or green tomato?

Call a vet or poison helpline promptly if the dog ate stems, leaves, vines, or a green tomato — solanine poisoning can cause drooling, vomiting, diarrhea, confusion, and weakness. A few bites of ripe red flesh is usually just a mild tummy upset that passes. Our feeding guide covers portion basics.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat ripe tomatoes?
Yes — ripe red tomato flesh is safe in small amounts, plain and without stems or leaves.
Can dogs eat tomato leaves or stems?
No — the green parts of the plant contain solanine and are toxic. Keep dogs away from tomato plants and their trimmings.
Can dogs eat green (unripe) tomatoes?
No — unripe tomatoes also contain solanine and can poison a dog. Only fully ripe red flesh is safe.
Can dogs eat tomato sauce or ketchup?
No — sauces and ketchup are high in salt and sugar and often contain garlic or onion, which are toxic. Only plain ripe tomato is safe.
How much tomato can a dog eat?
Keep treats under about 10% of daily calories: a couple of cubes for a small dog, up to half a cup for a large one.
Can puppies eat tomato?
Yes, a cube or two of ripe flesh once weaned and on solid food — watch for loose stools.
Is tomato good for dogs?
In small amounts, ripe tomato adds vitamin C, potassium, and lycopene, but it should stay an occasional low-cal treat.
What if my dog ate a tomato leaf or green tomato?
Call a vet or poison helpline promptly — solanine poisoning needs quick care. Watch for vomiting, drooling, or weakness.
