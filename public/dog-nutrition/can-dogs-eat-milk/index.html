Can Dogs Drink Milk? Usually Not Worth It

Most dogs should skip milk. Adult dogs are commonly lactose intolerant, and a bowl of milk more often leads to gas and diarrhea than any benefit.

Is milk good for dogs?

Usually not. Most adult dogs are lactose intolerant, so a bowl of milk more often leads to gas and diarrhea than any real benefit. A few laps will not harm most dogs, but it is not a healthy regular drink — which is why "is milk good for dogs" gets a qualified no rather than a yes.

Why does milk upset dogs?

As puppies wean, they lose much of the lactase enzyme that digests lactose, the sugar in milk. Without enough lactase, that sugar ferments in the gut and causes loose stools, gas, and sometimes cramping. Some dogs keep enough lactase to tolerate small amounts; many do not.

How much milk can a dog have?

  • A small sip now and then is fine for dogs that clearly tolerate it.
  • Skip it entirely if your dog gets gas or diarrhea from dairy.
  • Plain, unsweetened yogurt or a small piece of cheese is often better tolerated than milk, because these have less lactose and yogurt's live cultures can help.

Is milk safe for puppies?

No — not cow's milk. Orphaned or supplementary-fed puppies should get a proper puppy milk replacer, not dairy milk from the carton. Cow's milk has the wrong balance of fat, protein, and lactose for a puppy and can cause diarrhea and poor growth. Once weaned, the same lactose intolerance that affects adult dogs applies.

Puppies need their mother's milk or a proper puppy formula — never use cow's milk as a substitute, which lacks the right balance and can upset their stomach.

Better dairy options: yogurt and cheese

If you want to share a dairy treat, these are the safer choices:

  • Plain, unsweetened yogurt — lower in lactose and adds probiotics. A spoonful is plenty.
  • Small amounts of cheese — most dogs love it and tolerate it, but it is calorie-dense, so keep it occasional.
  • Avoid flavored, sweetened, or chocolate dairy products — chocolate is toxic and added sugars are unnecessary.

Signs your dog doesn't tolerate milk

Watch for loose stools, excess gas, stomach gurgling, or mild cramping within a few hours of drinking milk. If these appear, drop dairy and offer water instead. Persistent diarrhea deserves a vet check.

What about almond, oat, or soy milk?

These plant milks are not toxic, but they are not a good idea either. Almond milk is low in protein and often sweetened; oat and soy milks can contain added sugars or even xylitol, which is toxic to dogs, so always read the label. None of them offer nutrition a dog actually needs, and plain water remains the better choice. A tiny taste of an unsweetened, xylitol-free milk alternative is unlikely to harm, but there is no reason to make it a habit.

Frequently asked questions

Is milk good for dogs?
Usually not. Most adult dogs are lactose intolerant, so milk tends to cause gas and diarrhea rather than provide benefit. Water is the best drink.
Why does milk give dogs diarrhea?
Most adult dogs are lactose intolerant — they lose lactase after weaning, so milk sugar ferments in the gut and loosens stools.
Can dogs have yogurt instead of milk?
Plain, unsweetened yogurt is often better tolerated than milk and adds probiotics. Keep it to a spoonful.
Can puppies drink cow's milk?
No — use a proper puppy milk replacer. Cow's milk is unbalanced for puppies and can cause diarrhea and poor growth.
How much milk can a dog drink?
Only a small sip now and then, and only if your dog clearly tolerates dairy. Skip it entirely if it causes gas or diarrhea.
Is cheese okay for dogs?
Small amounts of plain cheese are usually fine and many dogs tolerate it, but it is calorie-dense, so keep it occasional.
What should dogs drink instead of milk?
Water. Every dog should drink water; milk is not a necessary or healthy regular drink.
My dog drank milk and seems fine — is it okay to keep giving it?
Some dogs tolerate small amounts. If there are no digestive signs, an occasional sip is fine, but it should never replace water.
