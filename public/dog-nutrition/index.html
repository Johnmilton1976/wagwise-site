DOG NUTRITION

Dog Nutrition — Feeding, Treats & Toxic Foods

What to feed your dog, how much, and which human foods are safe or dangerous. Covers toxic foods, safe treats, and daily feeding amounts by body weight.

GUIDE

Foods Toxic to Dogs — The List Every Owner Needs

The most dangerous are chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions and garlic, xylitol (in sugar-free gum), and alcohol. Even small amounts can be serious for a small dog.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Bananas? Yes — in Moderation

Yes, bananas are safe for dogs in small amounts. They are a healthy treat, but their sugar and fiber mean they should stay an occasional snack, not a daily staple.

GUIDE

How Much Should I Feed My Dog? (By Weight and Age)

Start from your dog's target weight and the food's calorie label, then adjust by body condition — you should feel ribs but not see them.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Apples? Yes — Seeds and Core Out

Yes, apples are safe and healthy for dogs in moderation. The flesh is fine; the seeds and core are the parts to skip.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Watermelon? Yes — Rind and Seeds Off

Yes, watermelon is safe for dogs — the red flesh only. Leave out the rind and the seeds, which can upset the stomach or cause a blockage.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Cheese? In Small Amounts, Sometimes

Most dogs can eat a small amount of cheese, but many are lactose intolerant and all should keep it occasional because of the fat and salt.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Peanut Butter? Yes — Read the Label First

Yes, plain peanut butter is a great dog treat — but only if it contains no xylitol, which is extremely toxic to dogs. Always read the label.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Carrots? Yes — A Near-Perfect Treat

Yes, carrots are one of the best treats you can give a dog: low in calories, high in fiber and vitamin A, and a crunchy chew many dogs enjoy.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Eggs? Cooked, Plain, and Moderate

Yes, cooked eggs are a healthy, protein-rich addition to a dog's diet. Raw eggs are best avoided because of salmonella risk and a biotin concern.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Chicken? Cooked, Plain, Boneless

Yes, plain cooked chicken is one of the safest, most common proteins for dogs. The risks are cooked bones and rich seasoning, not the meat itself.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Drink Milk? Usually Not Worth It

Most dogs should skip milk. Adult dogs are commonly lactose intolerant, and a bowl of milk more often leads to gas and diarrhea than any benefit.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Strawberries? Yes — Wash and Limit

Yes, strawberries are safe for dogs in moderation. They are low in calories and full of vitamin C, but their natural sugar means they should stay an occasional treat.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Grapes? NO — They Are Toxic

No — grapes and raisins are toxic to dogs and can cause sudden, severe kidney failure. There is no safe amount, so treat any ingestion as an emergency.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Chocolate? NO — It Is Toxic

No — chocolate is toxic to dogs because it contains theobromine, which they cannot metabolize well. Dark and baking chocolate are the most dangerous.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Avocado? Risky — Best Avoided

Avocado is best avoided. The flesh is the safest part but still high in fat; the pit is a choking hazard and the skin and leaves carry persin, which upsets the gut.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Blueberries? Yes — A Tiny Antioxidant Boost

Yes — blueberries are one of the safest, healthiest treats you can give a dog. They are low in calories and packed with antioxidants, and most dogs love the burst of sweetness.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Pumpkin? Yes — Plain, Cooked, and Helpful

Yes — plain cooked pumpkin (never the sugary pie filling) is one of the most useful foods to add to a dog's bowl. Its fiber helps firm up loose stool and ease mild constipation.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Sweet Potato? Yes — Cooked, Plain

Yes — plain cooked sweet potato is one of the better carbohydrate treats you can share. It is packed with fiber and vitamin A, but only once it is cooked and freed of skin, salt, and spices.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Rice? Yes — Plain White Rice for Upset Tummies

Yes — plain cooked white rice is one of the safest, most digestible foods for dogs, and it is the backbone of the standard bland diet vets recommend for mild tummy upset.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Cucumber? Yes — a Low-Calorie Crunch

Yes — cucumber is a safe, crunchy, near-zero-calorie treat that is mostly water, so it is a great low-fat option for dogs, including those watching their weight. Peel it, and skip the pickles.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Broccoli? Yes — in Small Amounts

Yes — broccoli is safe and genuinely healthy for dogs in small amounts. The catch is that too much can irritate the stomach and, in large doses, affect the thyroid, so portion control is the whole game.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Green Beans? Yes — a Low-Cal Diet Staple

Yes — plain green beans are a safe, low-calorie, high-fiber snack dogs can eat raw or cooked. They are a common diet food for overweight dogs, as long as they stay plain.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Pineapple? Yes — in Small Pieces

Yes — fresh pineapple is a safe, vitamin-C-rich treat in small amounts. The flesh only, cut into small pieces; skip the tough core, skin, and the sugary canned version.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Mango? Yes — Peeled, Pitted, Small

Yes — fresh mango flesh is a safe, vitamin-rich treat in small pieces. Peel it, remove the pit, and skip the skin; the pit is a choking hazard and the skin is hard to digest.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Potato? Cooked Only, Never Raw

Yes — fully cooked potato is safe for dogs, but raw potato is toxic and fried or buttered potato is off the menu. Plain, boiled or baked, and never green or sprouted.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Spinach? Yes — in Small Amounts

Yes — plain spinach is safe for dogs in small amounts. It is nutrient-rich, but the natural oxalates mean it should stay an occasional treat, not a daily heap.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Tomatoes? Ripe Flesh, No Leaves

Yes — ripe red tomato flesh is safe in small amounts, but the green parts, stems, and leaves contain solanine and are toxic. Skip sauces and seasoned tomato.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Bread? Yes — Plain, but Empty Calories

Yes — a small piece of plain, fully baked bread is not toxic to dogs, but it is mostly empty calories. The one real emergency is raw dough, which can bloat the stomach and ferment into alcohol.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Popcorn? Yes — Air-Popped and Plain

Yes — plain air-popped popcorn is a light, low-calorie treat many dogs love, but buttered and salted popcorn is not, and unpopped kernels are a choking and teeth risk. Plain and fully popped is the rule.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Honey? Yes — in Tiny Amounts

Yes — a lick of plain honey is not toxic to dogs, but it is essentially sugar, so it belongs in the 'tiny treat' category. The two dogs who should skip it entirely are puppies under a year and diabetic dogs.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Yogurt? Yes — Plain and Unsweetened

Yes — plain, unsweetened yogurt is a safe, gut-friendly treat for many dogs, thanks to lower lactose than milk and live cultures. The catches are lactose intolerance and, above all, any yogurt sweetened with xylitol.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Salmon? Yes — Cooked, Never Raw

Yes — cooked salmon is one of the best fish you can offer a dog, packed with omega-3s and lean protein. The one hard rule: never feed it raw, because raw salmon can carry a parasite that is fatal to dogs.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Shrimp? Yes — Cooked and Peeled

Yes — cooked, peeled shrimp is a lean, protein-rich treat many dogs love. The rules are simple: cook it, peel it, and skip the garlic butter and frying that make shrimp dangerous.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Peas? Yes — a Healthy, Low-Fat Veggie

Yes — plain peas are a safe, low-calorie, fiber-rich vegetable that many dogs enjoy, and they show up in plenty of quality dog foods. The catches are canned peas (too much salt) and whole pods, which can choke a small dog.

GUIDE

Can Dogs Eat Corn? Yes — the Kernels, Not the Cob

Yes — plain cooked corn kernels are safe and show up in many dog foods, but the corn cob is a serious hazard: it does not digest and can block a dog's intestine, often needing surgery. Kernels yes, cob never.

Good nutrition is the cheapest health insurance you can buy for a dog. Start with how much to feed, then learn which table scraps are safe and which can land you in the ER.
