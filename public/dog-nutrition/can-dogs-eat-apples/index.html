Can Dogs Eat Apples? Yes — Seeds and Core Out

Yes, apples are safe and healthy for dogs in moderation. The flesh is fine; the seeds and core are the parts to skip.

ADVERTISEMENT

Are apples good for dogs?

Apple slices are a crunchy, low-calorie treat full of vitamin C, vitamin A, and fiber. Many dogs love the texture, and they make a good training reward for dogs watching their weight.

How to serve them

  • Wash and slice. Remove the seeds and the core.
  • A few small slices for a small dog; a few more for a large dog.
  • Treats should stay under 10% of daily calories.
Apple seeds contain amygdalin, which releases cyanide as they are chewed. A seed or two is not a real danger, but do not let your dog crunch the core.

When to skip

If your dog has diabetes or a sensitive stomach, keep fruit minimal. Stop if you see an upset belly after a new treat.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat apple skin?
Yes — washed apple skin is fine and adds fiber. Just remove the seeds and core.
How many apple slices can a dog have?
A few small slices for small dogs, more for large dogs, and only as an occasional treat below 10% of daily calories.
Why remove apple seeds for dogs?
Seeds contain amygdalin, which can release cyanide; the core is also a choking risk.
