Can Dogs Eat Rice? Yes — Plain White Rice for Upset Tummies

Yes — plain cooked white rice is one of the safest, most digestible foods for dogs, and it is the backbone of the standard bland diet vets recommend for mild tummy upset.

Is rice good for dogs?

Plain white rice is about as gentle as food gets. It is low in fiber and fat and easy to digest, which is exactly why vets point to boiled white rice with plain boiled chicken as the standard bland diet for a dog with mild diarrhea or a sensitive stomach. It is not a nutrient powerhouse, but it gives a recovering gut an easy source of energy while things settle.

White rice vs brown rice

White rice is lower in fiber and the easier choice during active tummy trouble. Brown rice keeps more fiber and nutrients, so it can suit a healthy dog as an everyday carb — but that extra fiber can be too much for a gut that is already upset. Rule of thumb: white rice for an upset stomach, brown rice for a healthy dog's regular meals.

Rice nutrition at a glance

For scale, here is roughly what is in one cup of cooked white rice (about 158 g):

NutrientAmount per cup
Calories~205 kcal
Fat0.4 g
Carbohydrate~45 g
Fiber0.6 g
Protein~4 g

Low fat and low fiber are the features that make it a gut soother — not a nutrition supplement.

How much rice for a dog?

As a bland meal, a useful amount by body weight:

Dog weightPlain cooked rice
Under 5 kg1–2 tablespoons
5–15 kg1/4–1/3 cup
15–30 kg1/2 cup
30 kg and up1/2–1 cup, per vet guidance
Rice alone is not a complete diet. It is a short-term soother or a small part of balanced meals — a dog cannot live on plain rice long term.

How to serve it

  • Boil plain with no salt, oil, butter, or seasoning.
  • For an upset stomach, mix with plain boiled chicken (no skin, no bones, no seasoning) — the classic bland combo.
  • Let it cool before serving.

The real danger: table scraps and seasonings

Never feed a dog seasoned or leftover rice. Human rice often contains onion or garlic — both are toxic to dogs and can damage red blood cells — plus salt and oil a dog does not need. Only plain, unseasoned rice is safe.

Can puppies eat rice?

Yes — plain white rice is a common, easily digested bland meal for puppies with mild tummy upset. Keep portions small for their size.

Are there risks?

Plain rice is low-risk, but a few points matter: white rice spikes blood sugar quickly, so diabetic dogs need smaller portions (a vet may prefer brown rice or a different carb); long-term rice-only feeding is nutritionally incomplete; and any rice with onion, garlic, salt, or oil is off the menu. For the full list of dangerous additions, see our toxic foods guide.

When rice isn't enough

Rice is for short-lived, mild gut wobbles. Persistent vomiting or diarrhea, blood in stool, lethargy, or a painful belly are not "feed more rice" situations — they need a veterinarian. Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat white rice every day?
Short-term, as a bland diet, yes. Long term, rice alone is not complete — most calories should come from balanced dog food.
Is brown rice better than white rice for dogs?
Brown rice has more fiber and nutrients but is harder to digest; white rice is the better choice for an upset stomach, brown for a healthy dog's regular meals.
Can dogs eat rice with chicken?
Yes — plain boiled chicken with plain white rice is the standard bland meal vets recommend for mild tummy upset.
Can dogs eat fried rice or leftovers?
No — leftover rice often contains onion, garlic, salt, or oil, and onion and garlic are toxic to dogs. Only plain unseasoned rice is safe.
How much rice for a dog with diarrhea?
Small amounts of plain white rice with boiled chicken can help, but see a vet if diarrhea lasts more than a day or two.
Can puppies eat rice?
Yes — plain white rice is a gentle, easily digested bland meal for puppies with mild stomach upset.
Can diabetic dogs eat rice?
White rice raises blood sugar quickly, so portions should be small and cleared with your vet, who may suggest brown rice or another carb.
Can dogs eat plain rice cakes?
A plain, unsalted rice cake is not toxic and can be an occasional low-fat chew, but it adds little nutrition — keep it rare and unseasoned.
