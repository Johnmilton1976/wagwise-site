Can Dogs Eat Walnuts? No — Especially Black Walnuts

Walnuts are not recommended for dogs at all. English walnuts mainly pose choking and pancreatitis risk, but black walnuts can carry a mold toxin that causes tremors and seizures. Moldy walnuts of any kind are an emergency.

Short answer: no — and black walnuts are a genuine emergency. Walnuts combine the usual nut problems (fat, choking, poor digestion) with a risk many owners never think about: tremorgenic mold, which grows on walnuts — especially black walnuts — as they sit on the ground or in a damp garage bag. That mold produces toxins that cause tremors and seizures in dogs.

What tremorgenic mycotoxins do

Molds that grow on decaying nuts produce compounds that overstimulate the nervous system. Signs usually appear within 30 minutes to a few hours:

  • Muscle tremors that progress to full-body shaking
  • Panting, hyperthermia (dangerously high temperature)
  • Ataxia (wobbly, drunken gait)
  • Seizures in severe cases

A dog that eats moldy nuts — often stolen from a compost pile, yard, or garbage bin — needs veterinary care immediately. With prompt treatment, most dogs recover fully.

The everyday walnut problems

Even a fresh English walnut is a poor choice. Like almonds and macadamia nuts, walnuts are calorie-dense and fatty: a steady walnut habit contributes to weight gain and can trigger pancreatitis, and a whole nut can obstruct a small dog's intestine. Shells add dental fractures and blockage risk.

Walnut season hazards

If you have a walnut tree — or a neighbor does — autumn is the risky season. Fallen walnuts sit on wet ground and mold quickly, and dogs find them fascinating. Rake and remove fallen nuts before your dog is let out, and teach a solid leave it cue.

My dog ate a walnut — what now?

  1. Identify the type and condition: fresh store-bought, black, or visibly moldy?
  2. Estimate the quantity and whether shells were eaten.
  3. Fresh English walnut, one or two, large dog: monitor for 24 hours.
  4. Black walnut, moldy nut, many nuts, a small dog, or any tremor: call your vet or a pet poison hotline immediately.

For a safe crunchy snack, stick with carrots, apple slices, or plain air-popped popcorn — and keep the nut bowl for the humans.

Frequently asked questions

Are walnuts toxic to dogs?
Black walnuts and moldy walnuts can be genuinely toxic due to tremorgenic mycotoxins. Fresh English walnuts are not poisonous, but they are still unhealthy — high fat, choking risk, and possible pancreatitis.
What if my dog ate one walnut off the floor?
If it was a fresh, store-bought English walnut and your dog is large, watch for vomiting or diarrhea over 24 hours. If it came from a yard or tree and may have molded, call your vet.
Why are black walnuts worse for dogs?
Black walnuts are strongly associated with tremorgenic mycotoxins — poisons from mold that cause tremors, high fever, and seizures. They also drop from trees and mold quickly on the ground.
Can dogs eat walnut shells?
No. Shells can crack teeth, splinter, and cause intestinal obstruction. If your dog swallowed shell pieces, monitor for vomiting, constipation, or belly pain and call your vet if anything changes.
Is walnut oil or walnut butter safe?
Neither is recommended. The oil is pure fat (pancreatitis risk), and nut butters may contain xylitol or added salt. Stick to dog-safe treats.
What are the signs of moldy walnut poisoning?
Tremors that start within minutes to hours, wobbliness, panting, high body temperature, and possibly seizures. This is an emergency — go to a vet immediately.
Can puppies eat walnuts?
No. Puppies are smaller, so doses hit harder, and their digestive systems are more sensitive. The obstruction risk is also higher.
Which nuts can dogs eat safely?
None are truly 'safe' treats — peanuts are the least risky and plain, unsalted peanut butter (xylitol-free) is common in dog treats. But for crunch, choose carrots or apple slices instead.
