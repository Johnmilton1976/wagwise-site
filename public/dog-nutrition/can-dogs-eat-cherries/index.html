Can Dogs Eat Cherries? Flesh Yes — Pits Never

Cherry flesh itself is not toxic, but the pits, stems, and leaves contain cyanide compounds — and pits can also choke a dog or block the intestine. The practical answer: no cherries with pits, ever.

Short answer: the flesh, maybe — the rest, never. Cherry fruit itself is not poisonous to dogs, but every other part of the fruit — the pit, stem, and leaves — contains cyanogenic compounds that release cyanide when chewed and broken down. Pits also create a serious physical hazard: they are the right size to lodge in a dog's throat or intestine.

The cyanide issue, explained simply

Cherry pits and leaves contain cyanogenic glycosides — natural plant chemicals that convert to cyanide when the plant tissue is crushed or chewed. A single pit is unlikely to poison a large dog because the pit usually passes intact, but the risk is real when pits are chewed, when a small dog eats several, or when a dog gets into stems and leaves.

Signs of cyanide poisoning are rapid and severe: bright-red gums, dilated pupils, difficulty breathing, and collapse. This is an emergency — go to a vet immediately.

The pit is also a physical hazard

Even without toxicity, a swallowed cherry pit can obstruct a dog's intestine — the same life-threatening blockage caused by a corn cob. Small dogs are at the highest risk, and a blocked gut often needs surgery.

What about processed cherries?

  • Fresh cherries with pits — the biggest hazard; never hand a dog a whole cherry.
  • Pitted fresh cherry flesh — the only technically safe form, and even then, only in small amounts because of the sugar.
  • Maraschino and glacé cherries — sugar-loaded and sometimes alcohol-tinged; skip them.
  • Cherry pie filling and cherry yogurt — sugar plus flavorings; some products may contain xylitol, so check labels carefully.
  • Cherries in trail mix — usually dried, sweetened, and mixed with raisins or nuts, both worse hazards.

How much cherry flesh is okay?

If you do share pitted cherry flesh, keep it to a couple of cherries for a medium dog — fruit sugar adds up. Safer fruit treats with more reward and less hassle include blueberries, strawberries, and watermelon (seedless).

Dog weightOccasional pitted-flesh amount
Under 5 kg (toy / small)½ cherry, pitted
5–10 kg (11–22 lb)1 cherry, pitted
10–25 kg (22–55 lb)2–3 cherries, pitted
Over 25 kg (55 lb)3–4 cherries, pitted

In practice, most vets suggest skipping cherries entirely — other fruits give the same joy with none of the pit risk.

What to do if your dog ate cherries

  1. Figure out what was eaten — flesh only, or pits, stems, or leaves too, and roughly how many.
  2. Pits swallowed whole: call your vet to discuss monitoring for obstruction and whether decontamination makes sense.
  3. Pits chewed, or leaves/stems eaten: treat as an emergency — cyanide signs move fast.
  4. Watch for 24–48 hours: vomiting, loss of appetite, constipation, or straining can signal an intestinal blockage and need same-day care.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat cherries without the pit?
The flesh itself is not toxic, so a pitted cherry in small amounts is technically safe — but the sugar adds up and other fruits are better choices.
What happens if a dog eats a cherry pit?
A whole pit usually passes, but it can obstruct the intestine — especially in small dogs. Chewed pits can release cyanide. Call your vet with how many and whether they were chewed.
Are cherry stems and leaves dangerous?
Yes. They contain the same cyanogenic compounds as pits. Treat ingestion as urgent, especially if a dog chewed them.
Can dogs eat maraschino cherries?
No. They are sugar-loaded, sometimes alcohol-tinged, and add nothing nutritionally. Skip them.
Is cherry pie filling safe for dogs?
No. It is heavy in sugar and flavorings, and some cherry products may contain xylitol, which is highly toxic. Check labels and keep all pie filling away.
How many cherries are toxic?
Toxicity depends on whether pits are chewed and the dog's size. There is no established safe pit count — prevention is keeping cherries out of reach.
What are the signs of cyanide poisoning in dogs?
Bright-red gums, dilated pupils, rapid or labored breathing, and collapse. This is an immediate veterinary emergency.
My dog ate one cherry off the floor — what now?
Most likely fine, but watch for vomiting or behavior change over 24–48 hours and call your vet if anything seems off, especially for a small dog.
