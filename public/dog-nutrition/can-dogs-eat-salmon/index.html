Can Dogs Eat Salmon? Yes — Cooked, Never Raw

Yes — cooked salmon is one of the best fish you can offer a dog, packed with omega-3s and lean protein. The one hard rule: never feed it raw, because raw salmon can carry a parasite that is fatal to dogs.

Is salmon good for dogs?

Fully cooked salmon is excellent for dogs. It is a rich source of omega-3 fatty acids, which support skin, coat, and joint health, plus high-quality lean protein. Many premium dog foods use salmon as the headline ingredient for good reason. The benefits are real — as long as it is cooked and plainly prepared.

The danger: raw salmon

Never feed a dog raw salmon (or raw trout). In parts of the Pacific Northwest, wild salmon can carry a parasite, Nanophyetus salmincola, that harbors a bacterium causing salmon poisoning disease — fatal to dogs if untreated, though it does not affect people. Cooking kills both the parasite and the bacterium. Raw fish elsewhere also risks ordinary parasites and bacteria, so cook it every time.

Salmon nutrition at a glance

For scale, here is roughly what is in 100 g of cooked salmon (about one small fillet piece):

NutrientPer 100 g cooked
Calories~208 kcal
Protein~20 g
Fat~13 g
Omega-3~1.5–2.5 g

High protein and omega-3 with moderate fat are why cooked salmon is a standout topper, not just a treat.

How much salmon is safe?

Treats of any kind should stay under about 10% of a dog's daily calories. A sensible occasional amount by body weight:

Dog weightSafe occasional amount
Under 5 kg (toy / small)1–2 small flakes (1 tablespoon)
5–15 kg2–3 tablespoons
15–30 kg1/4 cup flaked
30 kg and up1/4–1/2 cup, occasionally

How to serve salmon

  • Always cook it through — baked, steamed, or poached. No raw.
  • Remove every bone; even small pin bones can choke or splinter.
  • No salt, oil, garlic, onion, or seasoning — plain is the rule.
  • Skip smoked salmon: it is far too salty for a dog.

Can puppies eat salmon?

Yes — cooked, deboned, plain salmon is a healthy protein for puppies in small amounts. It is gentler than many meats and supports growing coats and brains.

Are there risks?

Beyond raw salmon, the catches are bones and rich skin (high fat can trigger pancreatitis in sensitive dogs), plus any salmon prepared with garlic, onion, salt, or oil. Canned salmon is acceptable if it is in water with no salt added and bones removed. For more safe proteins, see our chicken guide and shrimp guide.

When salmon isn't the answer

Salmon is a healthy occasional protein, not an everyday staple, and it does not replace balanced dog food. Dogs with pancreatitis or fish allergies should skip it. If your dog shows vomiting, diarrhea, or lethargy after eating fish, our vomiting guide explains what is urgent, and a vet visit beats another fillet.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat salmon?
Yes — cooked, deboned, plain salmon is a healthy, omega-3-rich protein for dogs; it is a common premium dog-food ingredient.
Why can't dogs eat raw salmon?
Raw salmon can carry a parasite that causes salmon poisoning disease, fatal to dogs if untreated; cooking kills it. Raw fish also risks other parasites and bacteria.
Is salmon good for a dog's coat and skin?
Yes — the omega-3s in cooked salmon support skin, coat, and joint health, which is why salmon leads many quality dog foods.
How much salmon can a dog eat?
Keep it occasional, under about 10% of daily calories: a tablespoon for toy dogs, up to 1/2 cup for large dogs.
Can dogs eat smoked or canned salmon?
Smoked salmon is too salty; canned is OK only if packed in water with no salt and bones removed.
Can puppies eat salmon?
Yes — cooked, deboned, plain salmon is a healthy protein for puppies in small amounts.
Can dogs eat salmon skin?
It is fatty and can trigger pancreatitis in sensitive dogs, so it is best removed; plain cooked flesh is the safer choice.
What if my dog ate raw salmon?
Contact a veterinarian promptly — raw salmon can cause salmon poisoning disease, which is serious but treatable if caught early.
