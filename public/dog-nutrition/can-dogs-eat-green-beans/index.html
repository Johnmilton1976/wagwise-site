Can Dogs Eat Green Beans? Yes — a Low-Cal Diet Staple

Yes — plain green beans are a safe, low-calorie, high-fiber snack dogs can eat raw or cooked. They are a common diet food for overweight dogs, as long as they stay plain.

Are green beans good for dogs?

Plain green beans are about as healthy a treat as it gets: roughly 31 calories per cooked cup, with fiber, vitamin C, vitamin K, and manganese, and almost no fat. That makes them a go-to snack for dogs on a weight-management plan — many vets suggest them as a low-calorie swap for biscuits. The beans themselves are non-toxic; the problems come only from how they are prepared.

Raw vs cooked

Both raw and cooked are safe, but a whole raw green bean is a choking hazard, especially for small dogs that gulp food. Snap or cut them into bite-size pieces, and light steaming makes them easier to digest. Avoid frozen-for-cooking beans with added sauce.

Cut beans to match your dog's mouth. A full-length raw bean is the classic "went down the wrong way" item in the ER — short pieces only.

Green bean nutrition at a glance

For scale, here is roughly what is in one cup of cooked green beans (about 125 g):

Calories~31 kcal
Carbohydrate~7 g
Fiber~3.6 g
Vitamin Cgood source

Low calorie and high fiber are what make them a filling, diet-friendly bite.

How much green beans is safe?

Treats should stay under about 10% of daily calories. A sensible portion by body weight:

Dog weightSafe treat amount
Under 5 kg (toy / small)2–3 cut pieces (about 1 tbsp)
5–15 kgA small handful of pieces (2–3 tbsp)
15–30 kgUp to 1/4 cup cut pieces
30 kg and upUp to 1/2 cup cut pieces, occasionally

How to serve it

  • Plain, with no salt, butter, oil, or seasoning.
  • Raw (cut small) or lightly steamed — both are fine.
  • Let cooked beans cool before serving.

Can puppies eat green beans?

Yes, in small cut pieces once weaned and on solid food. Puppies have small airways, so keep the pieces tiny and watch them chew.

The real danger: butter, salt, and canned beans

Never feed green beans with butter, garlic, or onion, and skip canned green beans packed in salt. Garlic and onion are toxic to dogs, and the extra sodium and fat add real risk. Only plain beans are safe. For the full list of dangerous additions, see our toxic foods guide.

What if my dog ate too many?

A dog that raids a bag of plain beans mostly risks gas and a mild tummy ache that passes. Call a vet if there was butter, garlic, or onion involved, or if the dog is vomiting, lethargic, or shows signs of a blockage from swallowing whole beans. Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat raw green beans?
Yes, but cut them into small pieces first — a whole raw bean is a choking hazard, especially for small dogs.
Are green beans good for overweight dogs?
Yes — at about 31 calories per cooked cup and high in fiber, they are a classic low-calorie treat for dogs on a diet.
Can dogs eat canned green beans?
Only plain, no-salt canned beans, and even then rinse them. Regular canned beans are too high in sodium; avoid any with added sauce.
Can dogs eat green beans with butter or garlic?
No — butter adds fat a dog does not need, and garlic is toxic to dogs. Only plain beans are safe.
How much green beans can a dog eat?
Keep treats under about 10% of daily calories: a few cut pieces for a small dog, up to half a cup for a large one.
Can puppies eat green beans?
Yes, in tiny cut pieces once weaned and on solid food — keep them small to avoid choking.
Can dogs eat frozen green beans?
Yes — plain frozen (no sauce) beans, thawed or lightly cooked, are fine. Cut them small.
What if my dog ate too many green beans?
Plain beans usually cause just gas and a mild stomach ache. Call a vet if butter, garlic, or onion was involved, or for vomiting or lethargy.
