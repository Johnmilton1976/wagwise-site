Can Dogs Eat Macadamia Nuts? No — They Are Toxic

No — macadamia nuts are one of the most toxic nuts for dogs. Within 12 hours of eating even a small number, dogs can develop back-leg weakness, tremors, vomiting, and fever. There is no safe amount.

Short answer: no. Macadamia nuts are among the most toxic nuts for dogs, and unlike many kitchen hazards, the danger is not limited to a particular part of the nut — the whole kernel is poisonous. The exact toxin has never been identified, but the clinical picture is well documented: dogs who eat macadamias develop a distinctive combination of weakness, tremors, and fever.

What macadamia nuts do to dogs

Symptoms usually start within 3–12 hours of eating the nuts and typically include:

  • Hind-leg weakness — the classic sign; dogs wobble, stumble, or struggle to stand
  • Muscle tremors and shaking
  • Vomiting and abdominal pain
  • Elevated body temperature (fever)
  • Stiffness, lameness, or reluctance to move
  • In severe cases: inability to walk, collapse

Most dogs recover within 24–48 hours with supportive veterinary care, but severe cases — especially large ingestions or nut-and-chocolate combinations — can be life-threatening without treatment.

How many macadamia nuts are dangerous?

Very few. Toxicity has been reported at doses as low as roughly 1 gram of nut per kg of body weight — for a 10 kg dog that is only a couple of kernels. Chocolate-covered macadamias are doubly dangerous because chocolate brings its own, separate toxicity.

Hidden sources to watch for

  • Cookies, biscotti, and white-chocolate macadamia treats — a common bakery item dogs steal off counters
  • Trail mix and nut mixes — macadamias often travel with raisins and chocolate, both also toxic
  • Macadamia nut butter and oils — concentrated and easy to over-serve
  • Guest snacking and holiday baking — most exposures happen when visitors drop food

What to do if your dog ate macadamia nuts

  1. Estimate the amount — how many kernels, and whether they were chocolate-covered or mixed with raisins.
  2. Call your veterinarian or a pet poison hotline immediately, even if your dog seems fine — symptoms can take hours to appear and early decontamination works best.
  3. Keep the packaging so the clinic can confirm the product.
  4. Do not induce vomiting at home unless a professional directs you to.

Are other nuts safe?

Macadamias are uniquely toxic, but most nuts are still poor choices for dogs: they are high in fat (pancreatitis risk), often salted, and can cause choking. Moldy walnuts and pecans carry additional toxins. Safer crunchy treats are plain carrots or green beans.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat macadamia nuts in any amount?
No. Toxicity has been reported at doses as low as about 1 g per kg of body weight — a couple of kernels can sicken a small dog. Treat the safe amount as zero.
How quickly do symptoms appear?
Usually within 3–12 hours. Hind-leg weakness and tremors are the classic signs, often with vomiting and fever.
Are macadamia cookies dangerous too?
Yes — and white-chocolate macadamia cookies add chocolate and sugar on top. Contact your vet with how much was eaten.
Can a dog recover from macadamia poisoning?
Most dogs recover within 24–48 hours with supportive care, but severe cases need hospitalization — so do not wait it out at home.
What makes macadamias toxic to dogs?
The exact toxin is still unidentified, but the weakness-tremor-fever syndrome it causes is well documented by veterinary toxicologists.
Are other nuts safe for dogs?
Macadamias are uniquely toxic, but most nuts are high-fat, often salted, and a choking risk. Moldy walnuts and pecans add further toxins. Use carrots or green beans instead.
My dog ate one macadamia nut — should I worry?
Yes, call your vet. One kernel can matter for a small dog, and professional advice is free compared with late treatment.
Is macadamia nut butter or oil safe?
No. It is the same nut in concentrated form, and easy to over-serve. Keep all macadamia products away from dogs.
