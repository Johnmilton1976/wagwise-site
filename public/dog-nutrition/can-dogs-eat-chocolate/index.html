Can Dogs Eat Chocolate? NO — It Is Toxic

No — chocolate is toxic to dogs because it contains theobromine, which they cannot metabolize well. Dark and baking chocolate are the most dangerous.

ADVERTISEMENT

Why chocolate is dangerous

Chocolate contains theobromine and caffeine. Dogs break these down slowly, so they build up and overstimulate the heart and nervous system. The darker and more pure the chocolate, the more theobromine it carries — baking and dark chocolate are far worse than milk chocolate.

What to do if your dog eats chocolate

Call your veterinarian or an animal poison control line right away. Note the type of chocolate, roughly how much, and your dog's weight — these decide how serious it is.
  • Small amounts of milk chocolate may cause only mild upset in a large dog, but any dark or baking chocolate is urgent.
  • Keep all chocolate, cocoa powder, and baked goods well out of reach.

Watch for these signs

Restlessness, panting, a racing heart, vomiting, tremors, and in severe cases seizures. Symptoms can appear within a few hours and escalate, so do not wait.

Frequently asked questions

How much chocolate is toxic to a dog?
It depends on the type and the dog's weight; dark and baking chocolate are dangerous in small amounts. Any ingestion should prompt a vet call.
Is dark chocolate worse for dogs?
Yes — darker chocolate has far more theobromine than milk chocolate and is the most toxic.
What should I do if my dog ate chocolate?
Call your vet or animal poison control with the type, amount, and your dog's weight as soon as possible.
