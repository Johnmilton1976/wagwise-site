Can Dogs Have Caffeine? No — Coffee, Tea, Pills Are Toxic

No — caffeine in every form (coffee, tea, energy drinks, pills, grounds) is toxic to dogs. It speeds the heart and nervous system, and small dogs reach dangerous levels with just a lap or two of coffee.

Short answer: no — never, in any form. Caffeine is a stimulant that dogs metabolize far more slowly than humans, so it hits their heart and nervous system much harder. There is no safe sip of coffee, tea, energy drink, or soda — and some of the most dangerous exposures come from things dogs find on the floor: used coffee grounds, tea bags, and dropped pills.

What caffeine does to a dog

Caffeine overstimulates the heart and nervous system. Signs typically start within 30–60 minutes and can last many hours:

  • Restlessness, pacing, hyperactivity
  • Rapid breathing and racing heart rate
  • Muscle tremors and twitching
  • Vomiting and diarrhea
  • Elevated body temperature
  • Seizures and collapse in severe cases

How much caffeine is dangerous?

Mild signs can start around 20 mg of caffeine per kg of body weight, with serious toxicity near 40–60 mg/kg. To put that in scale:

SourceRough caffeine content
Brewed coffee (per 250 ml cup)~100 mg
Espresso shot~60–80 mg
Black tea (per cup)~45 mg
Energy drink (per can)~80–160 mg
Cola (per 330 ml can)~30–35 mg
Used coffee grounds (per tablespoon)~25–50 mg
Caffeine pill100–200 mg

A 5 kg (11 lb) dog can show mild signs from roughly 100 mg — a single cup of coffee or half an energy drink. That is why spilled mugs and trash-bin grounds are the most common real-world exposures.

Hidden caffeine sources to watch for

  • Coffee, espresso, tea, and energy drinks — including half-finished mugs
  • Used grounds and tea bags in the trash or compost
  • Caffeine and pre-workout pills — potent single doses
  • Cola and other caffeinated sodas
  • Chocolate-caffeine combinations — mocha desserts and coffee chocolate bars stack both toxicities (see our chocolate page)

What to do if your dog had caffeine

  1. Call your veterinarian or a pet poison hotline immediately — even if the amount seems small and your dog seems fine.
  2. Identify the source and amount — coffee vs. grounds vs. pills matters a lot for dosing.
  3. Note the time — early decontamination (within the first hour) is the most effective.
  4. Do not induce vomiting at home unless a professional tells you to.
  5. Expect monitoring — because caffeine acts over many hours, serious cases are watched at the clinic for tremors and heart rhythm.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs drink a little bit of coffee?
No. Even one cup can cause tremors and a racing heart in a small dog, and caffeine acts for many hours. There is no safe sip.
Is decaf coffee safe for dogs?
It is much lower in caffeine but not zero, and coffee compounds can still upset the stomach. Decaf is not a treat — stick to water.
Can dogs drink tea?
No. Black and green tea contain real caffeine, and herbal teas can contain plants that are also unsafe. Skip all tea.
What about energy drinks or cola?
Both contain caffeine plus large amounts of sugar — energy drinks can hold 80–160 mg per can, more than a cup of coffee. Never give either.
My dog ate used coffee grounds from the trash — is that bad?
Yes — grounds are concentrated caffeine and a common emergency. Call your vet or a poison hotline right away with how much and when.
How much caffeine is toxic to dogs?
Mild signs can start around 20 mg per kg of body weight; serious toxicity near 40–60 mg/kg. For a 5 kg dog, roughly one cup of coffee.
What are the signs of caffeine poisoning in dogs?
Restlessness, rapid breathing, racing heart, tremors, vomiting, and seizures — usually starting within 30–60 minutes.
Is caffeine bad for cats too?
Yes — cats are also sensitive and slower to metabolize it. Keep coffee, tea, and energy drinks away from every pet.
