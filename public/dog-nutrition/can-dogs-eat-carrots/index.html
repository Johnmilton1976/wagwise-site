Can Dogs Eat Carrots? Yes — A Near-Perfect Treat

Yes, carrots are one of the best treats you can give a dog: low in calories, high in fiber and vitamin A, and a crunchy chew many dogs enjoy.

ADVERTISEMENT

Are carrots good for dogs?

Carrots are a near-ideal dog snack. They are low in calories and fat, provide fiber and beta-carotene, and the raw crunch can help scrub plaque off teeth.

How to serve them

  • Raw: washed and cut into sticks or rounds. For small dogs, grate or soften to avoid choking.
  • Cooked: steamed or boiled plain — softer and easier to digest.
  • A few pieces as a treat; they still count toward the daily 10% treat budget.
Cut carrots to a safe size. A whole baby carrot can be a choking hazard for a dog that bolts food.

When to skip

Carrots are safe for almost every dog. If your dog has never had them, introduce a small piece and watch for any tummy upset.

Frequently asked questions

Are raw carrots okay for dogs?
Yes — raw carrots are safe and the crunch can help clean teeth. Cut them small to prevent choking.
Do carrots help dog teeth?
Chewing raw carrots can help reduce plaque, though they are not a replacement for brushing.
Can diabetic dogs eat carrots?
Yes, in moderation — carrots are low in sugar and a good low-calorie treat for diabetic dogs.
