Foods Toxic to Dogs — The List Every Owner Needs

The most dangerous are chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions and garlic, xylitol (in sugar-free gum), and alcohol. Even small amounts can be serious for a small dog.

ADVERTISEMENT

The big ones to never feed

  • Chocolate — the darker it is, the worse. Causes heart rhythm problems.
  • Grapes and raisins — can trigger sudden kidney failure, even a few.
  • Onions and garlic — damage red blood cells; toxic in quantity.
  • Xylitol — sugar-free gum and peanut butter; causes crashing blood sugar and liver injury.
  • Alcohol and raw dough — even small amounts are dangerous.
If your dog eats chocolate, grapes, xylitol, or anything on this list, call a vet or poison line right away. Do not wait for symptoms.

Usually fine in small amounts

Plain cooked chicken, rice, carrots, and apples (no seeds) are safe treats. The danger list above is the one to memorize.

What to do in an emergency

  • Note what and how much was eaten, and your dog's weight.
  • Call your vet or an animal poison hotline with those details.
  • Do not induce vomiting unless a professional tells you to.

Frequently asked questions

How much chocolate is toxic to a dog?
It depends on the dog's size and the chocolate type. Dark chocolate is far more toxic than milk. Any amount in a small dog should prompt a vet call.
Are grapes really that dangerous?
Yes — even a handful of grapes or raisins can cause acute kidney failure in some dogs. Treat any ingestion as serious.
Is xylitol in peanut butter?
Some brands use xylitol. Always read the label; choose a plain peanut butter with no xylitol for dog treats.
