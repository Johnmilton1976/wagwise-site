Can Dogs Eat Spinach? Yes — in Small Amounts

Yes — plain spinach is safe for dogs in small amounts. It is nutrient-rich, but the natural oxalates mean it should stay an occasional treat, not a daily heap.

Is spinach safe for dogs?

Plain spinach is non-toxic and genuinely nutritious — vitamin K, vitamin A, vitamin C, folate, iron, and fiber. The catch is oxalic acid (oxalates), which in large amounts can bind calcium and, over time, stress the kidneys, especially in dogs with existing kidney issues. The widely cited rule is the same as for any treat: keep spinach to a small, occasional amount and it is fine for healthy dogs.

Nutrient-dense, which is exactly why the oxalate caution applies — more is not better here.

The oxalate caveat

Spinach is high in oxalates. For a healthy dog, small occasional servings are fine, but dogs with kidney disease or a history of calcium oxalate stones should avoid it or get a vet's OK first. Keep portions small.

How much spinach is safe?

Treats should stay under about 10% of daily calories. A sensible portion by body weight:

Dog weightSafe treat amount
Under 5 kg (toy / small)A few small leaves or 1 tbsp cooked
5–15 kg1–2 tbsp cooked, chopped
15–30 kgUp to 1/4 cup cooked
30 kg and upUp to 1/3 cup cooked, occasionally

Raw vs cooked

Lightly cooked (steamed or boiled, no seasoning) is easier to digest and softer on the gut than a wad of raw leaves. Either way, chop fine so it is not a choking clump.

How to serve it

  • Plain, with no butter, oil, salt, garlic, or onion.
  • Steam or boil briefly, then chop small.
  • Let it cool before serving.

Can puppies eat spinach?

Yes, in tiny amounts once weaned and on solid food. Puppies have small kidneys too, so keep it a leaf or two and watch for any tummy upset.

What to avoid

Never serve spinach with butter, cream, garlic, or onion — garlic and onion are toxic to dogs, and the fats add needless calories. Only plain spinach is safe. For the full list of dangerous additions, see our toxic foods guide.

When spinach isn't the answer

Spinach is a small, healthy extra — not a meal and not a fix for a sick dog. Persistent vomiting or diarrhea, lethargy, or a painful belly need a veterinarian. Our vomiting guide explains what is urgent, and our feeding guide covers portion basics.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat spinach?
Yes — plain spinach is safe in small, occasional amounts for healthy dogs. It is nutrient-rich but high in oxalates.
Why is too much spinach bad for dogs?
Spinach is high in oxalates, which can bind calcium and stress the kidneys in large amounts, especially in dogs with kidney issues.
Can dogs with kidney disease eat spinach?
It is best avoided or cleared with a vet first, because the oxalates can worsen kidney and stone problems.
Is cooked or raw spinach better for dogs?
Lightly cooked (steamed or boiled, plain) is easier to digest; chop either fine to avoid a choking clump.
How much spinach can a dog eat?
Keep it occasional: a tablespoon or two for a small dog, up to a third of a cup cooked for a large one.
Can puppies eat spinach?
Yes, a leaf or two once weaned and on solid food — watch for any tummy upset.
Can dogs eat spinach with butter or garlic?
No — butter adds fat a dog does not need, and garlic is toxic. Only plain spinach is safe.
What if my dog ate a lot of spinach?
A healthy dog may just get an upset stomach; call a vet if there is vomiting, lethargy, or if the dog has kidney disease.
