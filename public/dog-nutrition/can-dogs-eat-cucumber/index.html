Can Dogs Eat Cucumber? Yes — a Low-Calorie Crunch

Yes — cucumber is a safe, crunchy, near-zero-calorie treat that is mostly water, so it is a great low-fat option for dogs, including those watching their weight. Peel it, and skip the pickles.

Is cucumber good for dogs?

Cucumber is about 96% water and very low in calories — roughly 8 calories in a half-cup of slices. That makes it one of the lightest treats you can offer, especially useful for overweight dogs or as a cooling snack on a warm day. It brings a little vitamin K, vitamin C, and potassium, but the real selling point is hydration and crunch with almost no fat or sugar.

Which part of the cucumber can a dog eat?

The flesh is the safe part. A few cautions on the rest:

  • Peel: the skin is where pesticide residue concentrates, and it can be a choking or gut irritant for small dogs. Peel it, especially if the cucumber is not organic.
  • Seeds: a few seeds are harmless, but they add bulk and can upset a sensitive stomach, so scoop them out for toy breeds.
  • Flesh: the safe, hydrating part — slice thin or into small cubes.
Wash the whole cucumber before cutting, even though you peel it — dogs lick the cut surface, so surface dirt and wax matter.

Cucumber nutrition at a glance

For scale, here is roughly what is in half a cup of sliced cucumber (about 52 g):

NutrientAmount
Calories~8 kcal
Water~96%
Carbohydrate~1.9 g
Fiber~0.3 g
Vitamin Ksmall amount
Vitamin Csmall amount

Low calorie and high water are the features that make it a diet-friendly crunch, not a nutrition supplement.

How much cucumber is safe?

Treats of any kind should stay under about 10% of a dog's daily calories. A sensible portion by body weight:

Dog weightSafe treat amount
Under 5 kg (toy / small)2–3 thin slices (about 1 tbsp diced)
5–15 kgA small handful of slices (2–3 tbsp)
15–30 kgUp to 1/4 cup sliced
30 kg and upUp to 1/2 cup sliced, occasionally

How to serve it

  • Peel, then slice thin or cut into small cubes.
  • Remove seeds for very small dogs.
  • For a cooling chew, freeze thin slices — a safe summer ice treat.
  • Offer plain. No salt, oil, dressing, or dip.

Can puppies eat cucumber?

Yes, in small amounts once they are weaned and eating solid food. Puppies have smaller tummies, so keep it to a slice or two and watch for loose stools.

The real danger: pickles and seasonings

Never feed a dog pickles, cucumber salad, or seasoned cucumber. Pickles are loaded with salt and often contain garlic or onion — both toxic to dogs — plus vinegar and spices a dog does not need. Only plain, unseasoned cucumber is safe.

What if my dog ate a pickle?

A small bite of a plain dill pickle is unlikely to be dangerous beyond extra salt, but if it contained garlic or onion, or the dog ate a lot, call your vet or a poison helpline. Watch for vomiting, diarrhea, lethargy, or excessive thirst. For the full list of dangerous additions, see our toxic foods guide.

Cucumber vs other crunchy treats

Cucumber is a lighter choice than many fruits. Watermelon is another safe, hydrating option (flesh only), while grapes and raisins must never be given because they are toxic. Keep treats small and see our feeding guide for portion basics.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat cucumber skin?
It is safest to peel it — the skin holds pesticide residue and can irritate a small dog's gut. Peel, especially if not organic.
Is cucumber good for overweight dogs?
Yes — at about 8 calories per half-cup, it is one of the lightest treats you can give a dog watching its weight.
Can dogs eat pickles?
No — pickles are high in salt and often contain garlic or onion, both toxic to dogs. Only plain cucumber is safe.
Can puppies eat cucumber?
Yes, a slice or two once weaned and on solid food — peeled, seeded for tiny breeds, and watch for loose stools.
How much cucumber can a dog eat?
Keep treats under about 10% of daily calories: a few slices for a small dog, up to half a cup for a large one, and only occasionally.
Can dogs eat frozen cucumber?
Yes — plain frozen thin slices are a safe cooling summer snack. Never add salt, sugar, or syrup.
Can dogs eat cucumber seeds?
A few seeds are harmless, but scoop them out for toy breeds to avoid an upset stomach.
What if my dog ate a pickle?
A small plain dill bite is usually just extra salt; if it had garlic or onion, or the dog ate a lot, call your vet and watch for vomiting or lethargy.
