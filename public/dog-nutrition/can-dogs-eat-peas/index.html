Can Dogs Eat Peas? Yes — a Healthy, Low-Fat Veggie

Yes — plain peas are a safe, low-calorie, fiber-rich vegetable that many dogs enjoy, and they show up in plenty of quality dog foods. The catches are canned peas (too much salt) and whole pods, which can choke a small dog.

Are peas good for dogs?

Green peas are a healthy, low-fat treat for dogs. They bring fiber, a surprising amount of plant protein, and vitamins like K and C plus thiamin. Because they are low in calories, they are a good crunchy option for dogs watching their weight — and they are a common, well-tolerated ingredient in premium dog foods.

The catches: canned peas and pods

  • Canned peas: almost always packed in salt, so the sodium is too high for a dog. Use fresh or frozen instead.
  • Pods and strings: snow or sugar-snap pea pods can be a choking or gut hazard for small dogs — shell them or cut into tiny pieces.
  • Raw garden peas: fine in small amounts, though cooking or thawing makes them easier to digest.
Frozen peas straight from the bag, thawed, are one of the easiest safe veggies — no salt, no prep beyond a rinse.

Peas nutrition at a glance

For scale, here is roughly what is in 100 g of cooked green peas (about half a cup):

NutrientPer 100 g cooked
Calories~81 kcal
Protein~5 g
Carbohydrate~14 g
Fiber~5 g
Vitamin Csmall amount

Low calorie, decent fiber and protein are why peas are a smart light treat, not a nutrition gap-filler.

How much peas is safe?

Treats of any kind should stay under about 10% of a dog's daily calories. A sensible occasional amount by body weight:

Dog weightSafe occasional amount
Under 5 kg (toy / small)1 tablespoon, mashed or whole
5–15 kg2–3 tablespoons
15–30 kg1/4 cup
30 kg and upUp to 1/3 cup, occasionally

How to serve peas

  • Fresh cooked or thawed frozen, plain — no salt, butter, or oil.
  • Shell snow/sugar-snap peas and cut pods small for toy breeds.
  • Mash for very small dogs so nothing is a choking lump.

Can puppies eat peas?

Yes — plain peas are not toxic to puppies and make a low-calorie training treat, but keep portions tiny and mash them for small breeds.

Are there risks?

Peas are low-risk, but they can cause gas in some dogs, and canned peas are too salty. A small number of dogs with kidney issues are sometimes advised to limit certain legumes, so check with your vet if that applies. When peas aren't the answer

Peas are a fun, healthy occasional veggie, not a meal. If your dog has persistent gas, bloating, or kidney concerns, our feeding guide and a vet visit beat a bag of frozen peas. Persistent vomiting or a painful belly needs a professional, not more vegetables.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat peas?
Yes — plain green peas are a safe, low-fat, fiber-rich treat for most dogs and are a common ingredient in quality dog foods.
Can dogs eat canned peas?
Avoid them — canned peas are packed in too much salt for a dog; use fresh or frozen peas instead.
Can dogs eat pea pods?
Snow or sugar-snap pods can choke small dogs, so shell them or cut tiny; the peas inside are the safe part.
Are frozen peas safe for dogs?
Yes — thawed frozen peas are one of the easiest safe veggies, with no salt and minimal prep.
How much peas can a dog have?
Keep it occasional, under about 10% of daily calories: a tablespoon for toy dogs, up to 1/3 cup for large dogs.
Can puppies eat peas?
Yes — plain peas are not toxic and make a low-calorie training treat, but keep portions tiny and mash for small breeds.
Do peas cause gas in dogs?
Some dogs get gassy from peas; start small and watch, and limit them if your dog is prone to flatulence.
Are peas bad for dogs with kidney issues?
A vet may advise limiting certain legumes for some kidney conditions, so check with your vet if that applies.
