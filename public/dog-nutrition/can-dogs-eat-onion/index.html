Can Dogs Eat Onion? No — Even a Little Is Toxic

No — onion is toxic to dogs in every form, with no safe dose. Its sulfur compounds destroy red blood cells and cause hemolytic anemia, often days after eating. Onion powder hiding in sauces and broths is the most common source.

Short answer: no. Onion is one of the most dangerous everyday foods for dogs. Unlike many "people foods" that are safe in moderation, onion has no safe dose — and the toxic compound survives cooking, frying, and even dehydration into powder.

Why onion is toxic to dogs

Onions belong to the Allium family (along with garlic, leeks, chives, and shallots). They contain sulfur compounds called organosulfides that, once absorbed, damage a dog's red blood cells. The cells become fragile and burst (a process called hemolysis), leading to hemolytic anemia — too few healthy red blood cells to carry oxygen.

The damage is not instant. Symptoms can appear within a day, but often take 2–4 days to show up, which is why owners frequently don't connect the illness to the food their dog ate days earlier.

Every form is dangerous

  • Raw onion — whole, chopped, or in salads.
  • Cooked onion — sautéed, roasted, in soups or stews.
  • Onion powder — the sneakiest form; it is concentrated and hides in spice blends, gravy mixes, bouillon, and many packaged sauces.
  • Caramelized or fried onion — still toxic, just tastier to dogs.
  • Leftover scraps and table scraps — a single discarded slice can sicken a small dog.

Cats are even more sensitive than dogs, but this page focuses on dogs.

How much onion is too much?

Veterinary toxicology references place the concerning dose around 15–30 g of onion per kg of body weight (roughly 0.5–1 oz per 2.2 lb). That sounds like a lot — but onion powder is far more concentrated, and small dogs reach the danger zone with just a teaspoon of powder or a few bites of a sauced dish.

Because powdered and cooked forms are concentrated and easy to over-serve, treat the safe amount as zero for every dog, of every size.

Signs of onion poisoning

Watch for these over the 2–4 days after exposure:

  • Lethargy, weakness, or reluctance to move
  • Pale or yellowish gums (signs of anemia)
  • Rapid breathing or elevated heart rate
  • Red or brown urine (broken red cells are being cleared)
  • Vomiting, diarrhea, loss of appetite
  • Collapse in severe cases

What to do if your dog ate onion

  1. Note what and how much — fresh, cooked, or powder, and roughly how many grams.
  2. Call your veterinarian or an animal poison hotline immediately. Do not wait for symptoms; early treatment (induced vomiting, activated charcoal, IV fluids, and blood monitoring) works far better than waiting.
  3. Bring the packaging if it was a packaged or powdered product so the clinic can assess concentration.
  4. Do not induce vomiting at home unless a professional tells you to — it is unsafe in some situations.

Onion vs. garlic: the Allium family

Garlic is onion's close relative and causes the same type of poisoning, though its toxic threshold is higher. Both are unsafe, and "a little bit" is never a safe bet. See our pages on grapes and chocolate for other common kitchen dangers.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat cooked onion?
No. Cooking does not destroy the organosulfides; cooked, fried, or caramelized onion is just as toxic as raw.
Is onion powder dangerous too?
Yes — and it is the most common culprit. Powder is concentrated and hides in seasonings, gravy, and sauces; a small amount can equal a large amount of fresh onion.
My dog ate a tiny bit of onion. Will it be okay?
Maybe, but don't gamble. Small dogs and powdered forms are highest risk. Call your vet with the estimated amount and follow their advice.
How long until symptoms appear?
Typically 2–4 days, sometimes within 24 hours. The delay is why prompt veterinary contact matters even when your dog seems fine.
Can onion kill a dog?
Yes — severe hemolytic anemia can be fatal without treatment. The risk is highest in small dogs and with concentrated powdered forms.
Are onion-flavored chips or snacks safe?
No. They contain onion powder or extract plus salt and fat, making them doubly unsuitable.
What about leeks, chives, and shallots?
All Allium family members are toxic to dogs and should be avoided entirely.
Can I give my dog bone broth with onion?
No. Many broths and bouillon cubes contain onion. Use only plain, onion-free broth, or check the label carefully.
