Can Dogs Eat Broccoli? Yes — in Small Amounts

Yes — broccoli is safe and genuinely healthy for dogs in small amounts. The catch is that too much can irritate the stomach and, in large doses, affect the thyroid, so portion control is the whole game.

Is broccoli safe for dogs?

Both the florets and the stalk are non-toxic and packed with good things — vitamin C, vitamin K, fiber, and antioxidants. The issue is quantity. Broccoli contains isothiocyanates, compounds that in large amounts can cause mild to severe gastric irritation in dogs. The widely cited rule of thumb is that broccoli (and any treat) should make up no more than about 10% of daily calories, and some sources note the isothiocyanate risk rises sharply above that.

For scale, here is roughly what is in half a cup of chopped broccoli (about 45 g):

Low calorie and high in vitamins — a genuinely healthy bite when the portion is small.

How much broccoli is safe?

Keep broccoli to about 10% of daily calories or less. A practical portion by body weight:

Dog weightSafe broccoli amount
Under 5 kg (toy / small)1–2 small florets (about 1 tbsp chopped)
5–15 kgA few small florets (1–2 tbsp)
15–30 kgUp to 1/4 cup chopped
30 kg and upUp to 1/2 cup chopped, occasionally
The 10% rule is the line. Push much beyond it and the isothiocyanates can cause drooling, gas, and an upset stomach — and very large amounts are a real concern for the thyroid.

Raw vs cooked

Both work. Lightly steamed broccoli is easier to digest and softer on the gut; raw florets give a satisfying crunch but are tougher to break down. Either way, cut into small pieces so a dog cannot choke, especially on the stalk.

How to serve it

  • Plain, with no butter, oil, salt, cheese sauce, or seasoning.
  • Steam or boil briefly, or offer raw florets in small pieces.
  • Chop the stalk fine — it is the most common choking hazard.
  • Let it cool before serving.

Can puppies eat broccoli?

Yes, in tiny amounts once weaned and on solid food. Puppies have sensitive tummies, so a floret or two and watch for gas or loose stools.

What to avoid

Never serve broccoli with garlic, onion, butter, or cheese sauce — garlic and onion are toxic to dogs, and the fats add needless calories and gut risk. Only plain broccoli is safe. For the full list of dangerous additions, see our toxic foods guide.

When broccoli isn't enough

Broccoli is a small, healthy extra — not a meal and not a fix for a sick dog. Persistent vomiting or diarrhea, lethargy, or a painful belly need a veterinarian, not more vegetables. Our vomiting guide explains what is urgent, and our feeding guide covers portion basics.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat broccoli raw or cooked?
Both are safe. Lightly steamed is easier to digest; raw florets are a safe crunch. Cut small to avoid choking.
Why is too much broccoli bad for dogs?
Large amounts of isothiocyanates in broccoli can irritate the gut and, in big doses, affect the thyroid, so keep it under about 10% of daily calories.
Can dogs eat broccoli stalks?
Yes, but chop them finely — the stalk is the main choking hazard. The florets are the easiest part to serve.
Can puppies eat broccoli?
Yes, a floret or two once weaned and on solid food — watch for gas or loose stools.
How much broccoli can a dog eat?
Keep it to about 10% of daily calories: a couple of small florets for a small dog, up to half a cup chopped for a large one.
Can dogs eat broccoli with cheese or butter?
No — cheese sauce and butter add fat a dog does not need, and never add garlic or onion, which are toxic. Only plain broccoli is safe.
Is broccoli good for overweight dogs?
Yes — at about 15 calories per half-cup and high in fiber, it is a filling, low-calorie treat in small amounts.
What if my dog ate a lot of broccoli?
Mild cases cause gas and an upset stomach that pass. Call a vet for repeated vomiting, lethargy, or if a large amount was eaten, since very high doses concern the thyroid.
