Can Dogs Eat Blueberries? Yes — A Tiny Antioxidant Boost

Yes — blueberries are one of the safest, healthiest treats you can give a dog. They are low in calories and packed with antioxidants, and most dogs love the burst of sweetness.

Are blueberries good for dogs?

Blueberries are one of the safest sweet treats you can hand a dog. A cup has about 84 calories and almost no fat, but it carries vitamin C, vitamin K, fiber, and a heavy load of antioxidants — the pigments that give the berry its deep blue color. Those antioxidants are why blueberries show up in senior-dog diets: research in aging dogs has studied antioxidant-rich foods for supporting memory and learning as dogs get older, though the effect in your own dog is modest, not a cure.

Blueberry nutrition at a glance

For scale, here is what is in roughly one cup (about 148 g) of blueberries:

NutrientAmount per cup
Calories~84 kcal
Fiber3.6 g
Vitamin C14 mg (about 16% of a person's daily value)
Vitamin K29 mcg (about 24% of a person's daily value)
Sugar (natural)~15 g

These are human nutrition figures, but they show why a few berries are a light, low-fat reward rather than a calorie bomb.

How many blueberries can a dog eat?

Treats of any kind should stay under about 10% of a dog's daily calories. A sensible portion by body weight:

Dog weightSafe amount
Under 5 kg2–3 berries, mashed
5–15 kgA small handful (5–8 berries)
15–30 kgUp to 10–15 berries
30 kg and upA generous handful, occasionally

How to serve them

  • Wash well to remove pesticide residue, then serve fresh or frozen.
  • Mash or cut berries for small dogs and puppies — a whole berry can be a choking hazard for a dog that bolts food.
  • Mix a few into meals, use them as training rewards, or freeze them for a cooling chew.
  • Keep it to plain berries. Skip blueberry muffins, yogurt coatings, and anything sweetened — and never anything with xylitol.
Frozen blueberries make a great summer treat, but count them toward the daily treat budget. A dog that gets a lot of treats still needs most calories from a complete dog food.

Can puppies eat blueberries?

Yes — a mashed berry or two is fine once a puppy is weaned and eating solid food. Their tiny tummies mean keep it to a bite, and watch for any loose stools.

Are there any risks?

Blueberries are not toxic, and serious reactions are rare. The real watch-items are choking in small dogs (solve by mashing) and the natural sugar, which means diabetic dogs should keep portions tiny and check with a vet. The bigger danger is the vehicle: baked goods, flavored snacks, and "sugar-free" products can carry fat, sugar, or xylitol, none of which belong in a dog's bowl.

What if my dog ate a whole pint?

A dog that raids a container of blueberries mostly risks a sugary belly ache — gas, loose stools, maybe a briefly upset stomach. That usually passes. Call a vet if there is repeated vomiting, lethargy, or if the berries came with something else (chocolate, xylitol sweetener). Unlike grapes and raisins, blueberries are not a poisoning emergency.

Blueberries vs other fruit treats

Blueberries sit alongside banana and strawberries as safe, low-calorie options, while grapes and raisins must never be given because they are toxic. For the full list of what to avoid, see our toxic foods guide. To keep treats in check, our feeding guide explains the 10% rule.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat blueberry skin?
Yes — the skin is where most of the antioxidants are, and it is safe. Just wash the berries first.
Can puppies have blueberries?
Yes, a mashed berry or two once weaned and on solid food; keep portions tiny and watch for loose stools.
How many blueberries can a dog eat?
A few for small dogs, up to a generous handful for large dogs, and only as a treat below 10% of daily calories.
Are frozen blueberries OK for dogs?
Yes — plain frozen berries are a safe cooling treat. Avoid anything sweetened or with xylitol.
Are blueberries good for senior dogs?
They are rich in antioxidants that studies have linked to supporting aging dog cognition, though the effect is modest, not a treatment.
Can diabetic dogs eat blueberries?
Small amounts may be fine, but the natural sugar adds up — check with your vet before making them a regular treat.
Can dogs eat blueberry muffins?
No — muffins bring sugar, fat, and sometimes xylitol or grape products. Stick to plain berries.
What if my dog ate too many blueberries?
Usually just a sugary stomach upset that passes. Call a vet for repeated vomiting or lethargy, or if other ingredients were involved.
