Can Dogs Eat Grapes? NO — They Are Toxic

No — grapes and raisins are toxic to dogs and can cause sudden, severe kidney failure. There is no safe amount, so treat any ingestion as an emergency.

ADVERTISEMENT

Why grapes are dangerous

Grapes and raisins can trigger acute kidney failure in dogs, and the toxic dose varies so wildly that no one can say how many is safe. Some dogs are fine after a few; others collapse after one. Because you cannot predict which dog will react, every exposure is treated as potentially serious.

What to do if your dog ate grapes

Call your veterinarian or an animal poison control line immediately. Do not wait for symptoms. Faster treatment — inducing vomiting and IV fluids — greatly improves the outcome.
  • Note how many and roughly when they were eaten.
  • Keep grapes, raisins, and foods made with them (bakery items, trail mix) out of reach.
  • Watch for vomiting, lethargy, reduced appetite, and reduced urination in the following days.

Watch for these signs

Early signs are vomiting and lethargy; later signs of kidney failure include reduced or no urination and weakness. By then the damage is advanced, so act early.

Frequently asked questions

How many grapes are toxic to a dog?
There is no known safe dose. Even one grape has caused kidney failure in some dogs, so any amount is an emergency.
Are raisins as dangerous as grapes?
Yes — raisins are dried grapes and just as toxic, and dogs may eat more of them.
What should I do if my dog ate a grape?
Call your vet or animal poison control now. Do not wait for symptoms to appear.
