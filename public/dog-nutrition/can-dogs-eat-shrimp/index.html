Can Dogs Eat Shrimp? Yes — Cooked and Peeled

Yes — cooked, peeled shrimp is a lean, protein-rich treat many dogs love. The rules are simple: cook it, peel it, and skip the garlic butter and frying that make shrimp dangerous.

Is shrimp safe for dogs?

Plain cooked shrimp is safe for dogs and is a genuinely lean protein — high in protein, very low in fat, and a useful source of vitamin B12 and selenium. For a dog watching its weight, shrimp is a better treat than most cheese or meat scraps. The problems come from how people usually eat it: raw, shell-on, or drowned in garlic butter.

The risks in a plate of shrimp

  • Raw shrimp: can carry bacteria and is harder to digest; cook it through.
  • Shells and tails: sharp and a choking or gut-blocking hazard — always peel and remove the tail.
  • Devein: the dark intestinal tract is best removed for cleanliness.
  • Garlic butter and frying: butter adds fat a dog does not need and garlic is toxic; fried, breaded shrimp is off the menu.
Never feed a dog raw shrimp, shells, tails, or anything cooked with garlic, onion, butter, or heavy oil. Plain boiled or steamed shrimp, peeled, is the only safe way.

Shrimp nutrition at a glance

For scale, here is roughly what is in 100 g of cooked shrimp (about 4–5 medium shrimp):

NutrientPer 100 g cooked
Calories~99 kcal
Protein~24 g
Fat~0.3 g
Cholesterol~189 mg

Very high protein, very low fat — that is why shrimp is a great lean treat, in moderation.

How much shrimp is safe?

Treats of any kind should stay under about 10% of a dog's daily calories. A sensible occasional amount by body weight:

Dog weightSafe occasional amount
Under 5 kg (toy / small)1 small shrimp, chopped
5–15 kg2–3 small shrimp
15–30 kg4–5 medium shrimp
30 kg and upUp to 6–7 medium shrimp, occasionally

How to serve shrimp

  • Boil or steam plain, with no salt, butter, or seasoning.
  • Peel completely and remove the tail and vein.
  • Chop for small dogs so nothing is a choking lump.

Can puppies eat shrimp?

Cooked, peeled shrimp is not toxic to puppies, but the choking risk from shells is higher for small mouths, so keep portions tiny and always chop and peel thoroughly.

Are there risks?

The main risks are shells, raw shrimp, and garlic-butter or fried preparation. Shrimp is also a shellfish, so a small number of dogs are allergic — introduce one piece and watch for itching, swelling, or digestive upset. When shrimp isn't the answer

Shrimp is a fun, lean occasional treat, not a nutrition source or a meal. Dogs with shellfish allergy or pancreatitis should skip it. If your dog shows signs of an allergic reaction — face swelling, hives, trouble breathing — that is an emergency; call your vet. Otherwise, our feeding guide covers everyday portions.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat shrimp?
Yes — cooked, peeled shrimp is a safe, lean, high-protein treat for most dogs; it is low in fat and a good source of vitamin B12.
Why must shrimp be cooked and peeled?
Raw shrimp can carry bacteria, and shells or tails are a choking and gut-blocking hazard, so always cook and peel it completely.
Can dogs eat shrimp with garlic butter?
No — garlic is toxic to dogs and butter adds unneeded fat; only plain boiled or steamed shrimp is safe.
How much shrimp can a dog have?
Keep it occasional, under about 10% of daily calories: one small shrimp for toy dogs, up to 6–7 for large dogs.
Can puppies eat shrimp?
Cooked, peeled shrimp is not toxic, but the shell choking risk is higher for small puppies, so keep portions tiny and chop finely.
Can dogs be allergic to shrimp?
Yes — shellfish allergy is possible; introduce one piece and watch for itching, swelling, or digestive upset.
Is fried or breaded shrimp safe?
No — the frying, breading, and seasonings add fat and often garlic or onion, so skip it entirely.
What if my dog ate raw shrimp?
Watch for vomiting or diarrhea; if symptoms appear or your dog seems unwell, contact your vet. Cooked shrimp only going forward.
