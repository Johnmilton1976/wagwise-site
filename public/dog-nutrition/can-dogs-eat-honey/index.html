DOG NUTRITION

Can Dogs Eat Honey? Yes — in Tiny Amounts

Yes — a lick of plain honey is not toxic to dogs, but it is essentially sugar, so it belongs in the 'tiny treat' category. The two dogs who should skip it entirely are puppies under a year and diabetic dogs.

Is honey safe for dogs?

Plain honey is not toxic to dogs, and a small dab now and then will not hurt a healthy adult dog. But honey is mostly sugar — about 17 g in a single tablespoon — so it is a treat, not a health food. Any 'immune boost' or 'allergy cure' claims you see online are not backed by veterinary evidence; think of honey as a sweet occasional nibble, nothing more.

The two dogs who should skip honey

Two groups should not eat honey. Puppies under about 12 months: their gut is not mature enough to handle the Clostridium botulinum spores honey can contain, which can cause infant botulism. And diabetic dogs: honey spikes blood sugar fast. For both, honey is off the menu.

Honey nutrition at a glance

For scale, here is roughly what is in one tablespoon of honey (about 21 g):

NutrientPer tablespoon
Calories~64 kcal
Sugar~17 g
Protein~0.1 g
Fat~0 g

Almost pure sugar is why honey is a rare treat, not a daily drizzle — the calories add up fast for a small dog.

How much honey is safe?

Treats of any kind should stay under about 10% of a dog's daily calories. A sensible occasional amount by body weight:

Dog weightSafe occasional amount
Under 5 kg (toy / small)A dab (1/4 teaspoon or less)
5–15 kgUp to 1/2 teaspoon
15–30 kgUp to 1 teaspoon
30 kg and upUp to 1–2 teaspoons, occasionally

How to serve honey

  • Plain, with nothing added — no sweeteners, no mix-ins.
  • A tiny dab can help a pill go down if your dog is a fussy taker (check with your vet first).
  • Raw honey carries the same puppy caution, so keep it away from young dogs.

Are there risks?

Beyond sugar weight gain and tooth decay from regular use, the only real danger is the puppy botulism risk above and any honey blended with artificial sweeteners — which can include xylitol, highly toxic to dogs. Plain honey only, and never for puppies or diabetic dogs. For the full list of dangerous additions, see our toxic foods guide.

Can puppies eat honey?

No — not under about 12 months. Their immature digestive system cannot be trusted with C. botulinum spores. Wait until adulthood, and even then keep it to a rare dab.

When honey isn't the answer

Honey will not cure allergies or coat conditions, and it is not a substitute for balanced food. If your dog has persistent skin, weight, or gut issues, our feeding guide and a chat with your vet beat a honey jar every time. Plain pumpkin is a far better everyday topper for most dogs.

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs eat honey?
Yes — plain honey is not toxic and a tiny amount is fine for a healthy adult dog, but it is mostly sugar, so keep it a rare treat.
Why can't puppies eat honey?
Puppies under about 12 months have immature guts that cannot handle Clostridium botulinum spores honey can contain, risking infant botulism.
Can diabetic dogs eat honey?
No — honey spikes blood sugar quickly, so diabetic dogs should avoid it entirely.
Is honey good for dogs' allergies?
No proven veterinary benefit — honey is sugar, not a cure; any allergy claims are unsubstantiated.
How much honey can a dog have?
Keep it tiny: a dab for toy dogs, up to a teaspoon or two for large dogs, only occasionally, under 10% of daily calories.
Can honey help my dog take a pill?
A tiny dab can help a fussy dog swallow a pill, but check with your vet first and use plain honey only.
Is raw honey safe for dogs?
For adult dogs yes, but it carries the same puppy botulism caution, so keep raw honey away from young dogs.
What if honey has sweeteners added?
Avoid any honey with added sweeteners, which can include xylitol — highly toxic to dogs. Plain honey only.
