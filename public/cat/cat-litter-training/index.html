Cat Litter Training — Faster Than You Think

Cats are naturally clean and most use a litter box with almost no teaching. The work is in the setup: the right box, the right litter, and a good location.

The good news: cats arrive pre-trained

Kittens learn to dig and bury from their mother by around four weeks, and the instinct to use loose substrate is strong enough that most cats will use any reasonable tray put in front of them. Litter training is not really training — it is setting up an environment that does not give the cat a reason to refuse.

Almost every "litter problem" is one of four things: the box is wrong, the litter is wrong, the location is wrong, or the cat is in pain. The rest of this page works through those in order.

The box: size, number and type

RuleWhat it means in practice
One per cat, plus oneTwo cats need three boxes. Three cats need four.
Length = 1.5 × body lengthNose to base of tail, then add half. Most commercial boxes are too small.
Open, not coveredCovered boxes trap smell and give a cat only one exit — a real problem in a multi-cat home.
Low entry for seniorsA high-sided box is a barrier for a cat with arthritis. Cut a doorway into the side.

Box size is the most commonly ignored rule. A cat needs to be able to turn around fully and dig without touching the sides. A box the length of the cat is a box the cat will eventually stop using.

Choosing the litter

Cats have preferences, and they are usually conservative. The safest choice is an unscented, clumping, fine-grain litter — closest in texture to sand, which is what the instinct is calibrated for.

  • Unscented. Perfumed litter is for humans. Many cats actively avoid it.
  • Fine grain clumping. Easy to dig, easy to keep clean.
  • Depth of 5–7 cm. Enough to dig and bury; too little and the cat cannot cover, which is stressful.
  • Change brand gradually. Mix the new litter into the old over a week.

Pelleted wood, tofu and crystal litters all work for many cats. If you are switching because of a problem, change one thing at a time so you can tell what fixed it.

Where to put it

A cat will not use a box it considers unsafe or disgusting. The placement rules:

  • Quiet — not next to a washing machine, boiler, or a door that slams.
  • Two exits from the room where possible. A box in a dead-end corner is a trap for a low-ranking cat in a multi-cat home.
  • Away from food and water. Cats will not eat next to where they toilet, and putting the bowl beside the box can reduce water intake — which matters for urinary health.
  • Not hidden in a distant basement. Convenience matters: a cat in a hurry will use the nearest acceptable surface, and if the box is two floors away, the bath mat becomes acceptable.

Training a kitten or a new cat

Start small. Confine a new kitten to one room with the box, food and bed for the first few days, then open up the house. A kitten that cannot find the box in a large house will not use it.

Use timing. Put the kitten in the box after every meal, after every nap, and after every play session. Those are the three moments a kitten needs to go. Scratch the litter gently with a finger to demonstrate.

Praise, never punish. If the kitten goes in the box, calm praise. If it goes elsewhere, clean it with an enzyme cleaner and move the waste into the box so the scent guides it — then place the kitten in the box immediately afterwards.

Never rub a cat's nose in waste or use a spray bottle. It teaches fear of the owner, not the location, and cats that are punished around the box often start avoiding the box entirely.

Maintenance: the part that prevents most problems

TaskFrequencyWhy it matters
Scoop solidsTwice dailyCats will refuse a box with waste in it — this is the number one cause of accidents
Stir the litterDailyStops clumps sticking to the base
Top up litterAs neededDepth drops as litter is removed with clumps
Full change and washEvery 2–4 weeksWash with unscented soap; avoid strong disinfectants

Two boxes scooped twice a day beats one box scooped twice a day, by a wider margin than most people expect. If you can only do one thing, add a box.

When a trained cat stops using the box

A sudden change in a cat that was reliable is a signal, not defiance. Work down this list in order.

Possible causeHow to check
Medical — cystitis, stones, blockageVet first, always. Straining with little output in a male cat is an emergency.
Dirty boxIs it scooped twice a day? Be honest.
Litter changeDid the brand, texture or scent change recently?
Box moved or replacedA new box, or a clean box that lost its familiar smell, can trigger avoidance
New stressorNew pet, new baby, builders, a move, a new cat outside the window
Multi-cat conflictIs one cat guarding the path to the box?
ArthritisSenior cat, high-sided box — can it step in comfortably?

Clean every accident with a biological enzyme cleaner, not a normal household spray. Residual scent tells the cat the spot is a legitimate toilet. A UV torch in a dark room will reveal marks you have not found.

Moving house with a cat

Set up one room first — box, food, water, bed, and something that smells of the old home. Keep the cat in that room for two or three days, then let it explore the rest of the house with the door open. Put a box on each floor during the first weeks. A cat that cannot find a box in an unfamiliar house will not wait.

Senior cats

Arthritis is the most under-recognised cause of litter box failure in older cats. The changes that help: a box with a low entry (or a cut-down side), one box per floor so the cat does not have to manage stairs, a softer litter if the cat's paws are sore, and a ramp to favourite resting spots. If a previously reliable older cat starts missing the box, ask the vet about pain relief before assuming a behaviour problem.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to litter train a cat?
Most cats and kittens learn within days with a well-placed, clean box — they are naturally inclined to bury waste. A kitten raised with a mother is usually already trained by four weeks.
Why is my cat pooping outside the litter box?
Usually a dirty box, a box that is too small or covered, a location it dislikes, or a medical issue. Rule out pain first, then fix the setup.
How many litter boxes do I need?
One per cat plus one extra. Two cats do best with three boxes in separate locations — not three boxes side by side.
What is the best litter for a cat?
An unscented, fine-grain clumping litter, about 5–7 cm deep. Most cats avoid perfumed litters and prefer a sand-like texture.
Should I use a covered litter box?
Usually not. Covered boxes trap odour and leave only one exit, which is a real problem in homes with more than one cat.
Why did my cat suddenly stop using the litter box?
A sudden change in a reliable cat is usually medical — cystitis, stones or pain — or a new stressor. See a vet first, then check litter, box and location.
How often should I change the litter completely?
Every two to four weeks, with daily scooping of solids twice a day. Wash the box with unscented soap rather than strong disinfectants.
How do I stop my cat peeing outside the box after cleaning?
Clean with a biological enzyme cleaner, not household spray. Residual scent marks the spot as a toilet and invites repeat use.
