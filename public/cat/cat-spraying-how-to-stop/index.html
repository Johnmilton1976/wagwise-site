How to Stop a Cat Spraying Indoors

Spraying is your cat marking territory with urine on vertical surfaces — different from litter accidents. The fix is reducing stress and conflict, not punishment.

ADVERTISEMENT

Spraying vs accidents

Spraying is a small amount on a wall or chair back, often with a quiver tail. Litter accidents are on the floor. They have different causes and fixes.

Common triggers

  • Other cats — outside the window or in the home.
  • Stress from moves, new pets, or routine changes.
  • Unneutered males and unspayed females spray most.
First, a vet check — urinary pain can look like spraying. Once medical causes are out, address stress and marking.

What helps

  • Neuter or spay — cuts spraying dramatically.
  • Use synthetic pheromone diffusers to lower anxiety.
  • Block the view of outdoor cats and add vertical safe spaces.
  • Clean marks with an enzyme cleaner so the scent does not invite re-marking.

Frequently asked questions

Is cat spraying the same as peeing outside the box?
No. Spraying is a small amount on vertical surfaces for marking; accidents are on the floor, usually medical or litter-related.
Will neutering stop my cat spraying?
Often yes, especially in males — neutering removes most hormone-driven marking. Established habits may need extra steps.
How do I clean cat spray?
Use an enzyme cleaner, not regular cleaner, so the scent that triggers re-marking is fully removed.
