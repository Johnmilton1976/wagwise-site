How to Stop a Cat Scratching Furniture

Scratching is normal cat behavior you cannot train away — but you can redirect it. Give appealing posts and make the furniture boring.

Why cats scratch

It marks territory (visually and with scent), stretches muscles, and removes old claw sheaths. Declawing is not a fix — it is amputation and is banned in many places for good reason.

Redirect, don't punish

  • Place a tall, sturdy scratching post right next to the furniture they target.
  • Use catnip or silvervine on the post to draw them in.
  • Praise and treat when they use it.
Make targeted furniture uninviting: double-sided tape, a couch cover, or a citrus scent cats dislike. The post must be more attractive than the sofa.

Protect the claws another way

Soft plastic claw caps and regular nail trims reduce damage while you train. They do not stop the need to scratch, so posts stay essential.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stop my cat scratching the couch?
Put an appealing post beside the couch, use catnip, and make the couch boring with tape or a cover. Reward post use.
Should I declaw my cat?
No. Declawing removes the last bone of each toe and causes lasting pain. Redirect scratching instead.
Do claw caps work?
Soft caps reduce furniture damage and are safe, but they do not replace the cat's need to scratch — keep the posts.
