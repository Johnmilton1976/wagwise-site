CAT CARE

Cat Care Guides — Behaviour, Health & Everyday Problems

Practical cat care guides: litter box training, furniture scratching, spraying, hairballs, and night-time meowing — with the medical causes to rule out first.

Cats are not small dogs

Almost every cat problem in this section comes from applying dog logic to a cat. A dog that is shut out of a room is mildly annoyed; a cat that is shut out of a room may start urinating on the bed, because a cat's sense of security is built on territory and routine rather than on social approval. Cats are not stubborn and they are not vengeful — they are specific about conditions, and when the conditions are wrong they show it through behaviour and through hiding.

The three complaints behind most cat questions

ProblemMost common real causeFirst step
Peeing outside the boxMedical, in a large share of cases — cystitis, crystals, arthritis making the box painful to climb intoVet visit before behaviour work; a urine sample is the cheapest test you will run
Scratching furnitureNo acceptable scratching surface in the right place, or one that movesA sturdy vertical post next to the scratched object, not in another room
Meowing at nightUnspent energy, hunger timing, or a medical cause in older catsPlay-and-feed sequence before bed; bloodwork if the cat is over ten

The order matters. Cats hide illness well, and a behavioural explanation is the more comfortable one to reach for, which is why it is often the wrong one. Litter box avoidance in particular should be treated as a medical question until a vet has ruled it out — the behaviour work comes after, not instead.

The stress signals you are probably missing

Cats advertise stress quietly. Hiding more than usual, eating only at night, a coat that has stopped being groomed, over-grooming a patch bald, sitting with the head pressed against a wall, and a tail that flicks while the cat is supposedly resting are all reports worth acting on. A cat that has stopped jumping onto the bed is often a cat with joint pain, not a cat that has gone off you.

Change is the other trigger. A new baby, a new flatmate, a new cat, moved furniture or a building site next door will all show up as behaviour before it shows up as anything else. Cats do not need the change removed; they need somewhere to retreat to, resources in more than one place, and time.

What a cat actually needs set up

The rule of thumb that resolves most multi-cat friction: one litter box per cat plus one, spread across different rooms rather than lined up together. Food and water in separate places, because cats prefer not to drink where they eat. Somewhere high to sit, because vertical space is how a cat controls its own exposure. And a scratching post that is tall enough for the cat to stretch fully — most bought posts are too short, which is why the sofa wins.

None of this is enrichment for its own sake. Each item removes a reason for the behaviour that brought you to this page.

When it is not behavioural at all

See a vet rather than adjusting the house if: a cat stops eating for more than 24 hours (in cats this is a genuine emergency, not a hunger strike), drinks and urinates noticeably more, loses weight while eating normally, has a sudden change in litter box habits after years of consistency, or starts vocalising at night when it never used to. Those five are the classic presentations of the conditions that are treatable early and difficult late.

Cat Litter Training — Faster Than You Think

Cats are naturally clean and most use a litter box with almost no teaching. The work is in the setup: the right box, the right litter, and a good location.

GUIDE

How to Stop a Cat Scratching Furniture

Scratching is normal cat behavior you cannot train away — but you can redirect it. Give appealing posts and make the furniture boring.

GUIDE

How to Stop a Cat Spraying Indoors

Spraying is your cat marking territory with urine on vertical surfaces — different from litter accidents. The fix is reducing stress and conflict, not punishment.

GUIDE

Cat Hairballs — What's Normal and When to Worry

A hairball now and then is normal grooming housekeeping. The pattern is what matters — how often, how easily, and whether your cat is otherwise fine.

GUIDE

Why Does My Cat Meow at Night — and How to Make It Stop

A cat yelling at your bedroom door at 3 a.m. is not being difficult. The cause is usually energy, hunger, or a habit you accidentally trained — and the fix is a routine, not a punishment.
