Best Dog Crate 2026 — Practical Picks, Not Hype

The best crate is the one your dog accepts and you can clean easily. We prefer sturdy wire crates with a divider for puppies and a secure plastic crate for travel.

How these picks were made — and what they are not

No crate was bought, destroyed, or crash-tested for this page. There is no laboratory here and nobody on the team is a veterinary behaviourist.

What these picks are based on: published internal dimensions against the sizing rule below, the failure modes that show up repeatedly in owner reports and veterinary behaviour literature (latch failure, bar spacing, floor tray collapse, plastic becoming brittle in cold), and the ergonomics of actually cleaning a crate twice a week for a year.

That is a real basis for a recommendation, but it is not the same as a physical test, and we would rather say so than imply otherwise.

The sizing rule that decides everything

Most crate problems start with the wrong size, and most wrong sizes are too big rather than too small.

MeasurementHow to take itWhat the crate needs
LengthNose to base of tail, standingAdd 5–10 cm so the dog can turn around
HeightFloor to top of head, standing normally, ears upAdd 5–10 cm so the head clears when standing
WidthWidest point across the shouldersEnough to lie down fully stretched on one side

Now the part that matters for puppies: buy the adult size and use a divider panel. A puppy in a crate sized for its current body will need a second crate within months. A puppy in an adult crate without a divider will toilet in one corner and sleep in the other, which defeats the entire point of crate training.

A divider is not an accessory — it is the feature that makes an adult crate usable for a puppy. If a crate does not offer one, it is the wrong crate for a puppy.

Our picks by need

Best dog crate for a puppy: wire, with a divider

A wire crate with a divider panel and a removable floor tray. Shrink it now, expand as the dog grows, no second purchase. Look for bar spacing under 4 cm for a small breed — a puppy can squeeze its head between wider bars.

Best dog crate for an anxious dog: solid plastic

A solid plastic crate, because it feels more den-like and blocks visual triggers. The trade-off is ventilation and heat: a plastic crate in direct sun or a warm car is a genuine risk, so never use one as a sun shelter.

Best dog crate for car travel: crash-tested and anchored

A crash-tested plastic crate, anchored with a seatbelt or a strap through the crate's designated anchor points. "Crash tested" should mean tested to a published standard — not a marketing phrase on the box.

Best dog crate for home and travel together: two crates, not one

A wire crate for the house plus a soft travel crate only if the dog is not a chewer. A determined chewer will go through fabric in minutes, and the zips are the first thing to fail.

The failure modes worth checking before you buy

PartWhat goes wrongWhat to look for
Door latchThe single most common escape point; a sliding bolt that a paw can nudgeTwo-point latching, or a bolt with a secondary lock
Bar spacingA puppy's head or jaw can lodge between barsUnder 4 cm for small breeds and puppies
Floor trayThin plastic cracks, then the dog stands on wireA rigid tray that slides out without lifting the crate
Wire gaugeThin bars bend outward under a determined dogThicker gauge on the door panel specifically — that is where the load is
CoatingCheap powder coat chips, then rustsEven coating at the weld points, which is where it usually fails first
CollapsibilityFolding mechanisms that do not lock positivelyA crate that clicks into place rather than one that relies on friction

What we skip, and why

Furniture crates. Attractive, expensive, usually poor ventilation, and the enclosed design makes cleaning harder. A dog that is warm and bored will chew the wooden frame. Pretty is the last thing to optimise.

Very cheap crates with single-point latches. The cheapest crate that fails its latch costs more than a good one once the dog is loose in the house at night.

"Indestructible" crates marketed for escape artists. Some are genuinely stronger, but no crate substitutes for addressing the separation anxiety that drives the escaping. A dog that has broken out of a standard crate needs a behaviour plan, not a thicker crate.

Crate training is a separate skill from buying the crate

A good crate used wrongly produces a dog that fears it. Three rules that matter more than the product:

The crate is never punishment. A dog sent to the crate after misbehaving learns that the crate predicts bad things, and the one place you need it to be safe becomes the place it avoids.

Feed in it, with the door open. For the first week, meals go in the crate and the door stays open. Duration comes later.

Do not use it for long confinement. A crate is a management tool for short periods and overnight, not a substitute for exercise and company. A dog crated for a full working day is a welfare problem regardless of how good the crate is.

Bottom line

Buy once: a size-adjustable wire crate with a divider and a rigid tray for the house, and a crash-tested travel crate only if you drive with your dog regularly. Spend the effort on sizing and on the first week of training — those two things decide whether the crate works, far more than the brand does.

Frequently asked questions

What size crate does my dog need?
Big enough to stand, turn, and lie down. Measure nose to tail base and floor to head, then add 5–10 cm to each. For a puppy, buy the adult size and use a divider to keep the space snug now.
Wire or plastic crate?
Wire for home — airflow, visibility, divider options, easier cleaning. Plastic for anxious dogs and car travel, where the enclosed feel is an advantage and crash testing matters.
Do I need a crate for my dog?
Not required, but it speeds house training and gives a dog a safe space. Many dogs come to choose it voluntarily when it is introduced properly.
Is it cruel to crate a dog?
Not for short periods and overnight, when the dog is trained to it and the crate is the right size. Long daily confinement without exercise or company is a welfare problem.
Should I put a bed in the crate?
Yes, once the dog is not chewing it. A flat bed or vet-bed lining is safer than a thick cushion for a puppy that shreds things.
How do I stop my dog escaping the crate?
Check the latch first — most escapes are latch failures, not strength. Then look at whether separation anxiety is the real cause, because no crate fixes that.
Can a crate be too big?
For a puppy, yes. Too much space lets the puppy toilet in one corner and sleep in another, which undoes house training. Use a divider.
