Best Dog Crate 2026 — Practical Picks, Not Hype

The best crate is the one your dog accepts and you can clean easily. We prefer sturdy wire crates with a divider for puppies and a secure plastic crate for travel.

ADVERTISEMENT

What actually matters

  • Correct size — stand/turn/lie down room, with a divider to grow into for puppies.
  • Secure door latch — escape-proof for anxious dogs.
  • Easy cleanup — a removable tray saves you.

Our picks by need

For puppies: a wire crate with a divider panel — shrink it now, expand as they grow, no second purchase. For anxious dogs: a solid plastic crate feels more den-like and blocks visual triggers. For car travel: a crash-tested plastic crate anchored with a seatbelt.

Avoid crates with sharp edges or flimsy latches. The cheapest crate that fails its latch costs more than a good one when your dog gets loose.

What we skip

Fancy furniture crates are pretty but often poor ventilation and hard to clean. Pretty is the last thing to optimize.

Bottom line

Buy once: a size-adjustable wire crate for home, and a travel crate only if you drive with your dog often.

Frequently asked questions

What size crate does my dog need?
Big enough to stand, turn, and lie down. For a puppy, choose the adult size and use a divider to keep the space snug now.
Wire or plastic crate?
Wire for home (airflow, visibility, divider options); plastic for anxious dogs and car travel (cozier, more secure).
Do I need a crate for my dog?
Not required, but it speeds house training and gives a safe space. Many dogs come to love theirs.
