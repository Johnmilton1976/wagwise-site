Chocolate toxicity calculator for dogs

How much chocolate is dangerous depends on three things: your dog's weight, the type of chocolate, and how much of it went down. This calculator turns those into the number a vet actually asks for — milligrams of theobromine per kilogram of dog.

How much chocolate is toxic to a dog?

The toxic dose is not measured in squares of chocolate. It is measured in theobromine per kilogram of body weight, and the amount of theobromine in a gram of chocolate varies by a factor of about 3,000 between white chocolate and cocoa powder. That is why "my dog ate some chocolate" spans everything from a stomach ache to a seizure in the next six hours, and why the answer to this question is always two numbers: the dose and the ratio.

Dogs metabolise theobromine far more slowly than people do — the half-life in dogs is roughly 17.5 hours against two to three in humans — so the dose stacks up rather than clearing overnight. A dose that would be a strong coffee for you is a 12-hour hospitalisation for a Chihuahua.

The four thresholds vets work from

These are the bands used in small-animal toxicology. They are cumulative: a dose above 60 mg/kg has already passed through the two lower bands.

Dose of theobromineWhat it usually producesWhat to do
Under 10 mg/kgNothing in most dogs; a sensitive or small dog may have loose stoolWatch for 12 hours, water available, no need to phone unless something changes
10–20 mg/kgFirst mild signs are reported here in some dogs — vomiting, thirst, restlessnessPhone your vet for advice; most cases are managed at home with observation
20–40 mg/kgVomiting, diarrhoea, excessive thirst, hyperactivity, and a raised heart rateVet visit the same day. Vets may induce vomiting if it is inside two hours
40–60 mg/kgCardiac effects: tachycardia, arrhythmia. This is the band where dogs are admittedEmergency clinic. Do not wait for symptoms
Over 60 mg/kgSeizures, muscle tremor, hyperthermia, coma. The published lethal range starts around 100–200 mg/kgEmergency clinic immediately, in the car, now

Why the bands look lower than some charts. A few online tables only start at 20 mg/kg, because that is where clinical signs become common in a study population. Individual dogs vary, and the cost of treating the 10–20 band as "nothing to worry about" is a dog that is vomiting by midnight. The calculator flags that band as "watch", not "safe".

Read a row across, not down. A 10 kg dog needs about 129 g of milk chocolate to reach the 20 mg/kg band — roughly four and a half ounces, or one and a half standard 90 g bars. The same dog reaches the same band on 14.5 g of baking chocolate, which is less than half a square. Same species, same night, two completely different afternoons.

What to do in the next ten minutes

In this order, because the order matters:

  1. Do not induce vomiting unless a vet tells you to. Chocolate is not a caustic, but vomiting has its own risks, the window for it is roughly two hours, and it is not the right move for every dog — particularly a brachycephalic breed or a dog that is already drowsy or twitching.
  2. Find the packaging. The wrapper gives the cocoa percentage and the net weight, which converts the calculator's estimate into a real number. "A bar of chocolate" is not a dose; "90 g of 70% cocoa" is.
  3. Weigh the dog if you can, guess honestly if you cannot. Owners underestimate small dogs and overestimate large ones. A 10% error in weight rarely changes the decision; being wrong by a factor of two does.
  4. Phone with the four numbers: body weight, product and cocoa percentage, grams eaten, and how long ago. The vet needs all four to decide whether to see the dog.
  5. Watch for the first signs — restlessness, drinking more than usual, vomiting, a racing heart you can feel through the chest wall. Signs usually start within 6 to 12 hours and can persist for up to 72 because of that long half-life.

Why "dark chocolate is fine, it's the sugar that's bad" is backwards

The darker the chocolate, the more cocoa solids, and cocoa solids are where the theobromine is. The sugar and fat in milk chocolate are a calorie problem; the cocoa is the poison problem. Per gram, baking chocolate carries roughly nine times the theobromine of milk chocolate, and milk chocolate about 176 times what white chocolate does. That is why a single square of the 90% bar in the cupboard is a bigger event than half an Easter egg.

Two other things catch owners out. Cocoa powder and drinking chocolate are the most concentrated form in a normal kitchen — a spoonful in a mug is a serious dose for a small dog, and cocoa powder is also the one product whose label almost never states a percentage. Cocoa bean mulch in the garden is the same compound in a form dogs find interesting; it is covered on the full chocolate guide.

What the vet will do with these numbers

If the dog is inside two hours of eating and the dose is above roughly 20 mg/kg, many clinics induce vomiting with apomorphine or hydrogen peroxide under supervision, then give activated charcoal to limit absorption. Above 40 mg/kg expect fluids, a heart monitor, and possibly a beta-blocker for the tachycardia; the tremors and seizures are managed with benzodiazepines. Most dogs that are treated in the 20–40 band go home the same day. Most of the deaths in the literature are in dogs that reached the top band before anyone was called.

Sources and review

The theobromine figures behind this calculator are the per-ounce values published in veterinary toxicology references — 0.25 mg/oz for white chocolate, 44 for milk, 150 for semi-sweet, 390 for baking chocolate and 737 for dry cocoa powder — with the clinical bands from the same literature. Where ranges exist in the sources, the calculator uses the lower end, because under-estimating a chocolate dose is the direction that gets a dog seen.

Quick answers

How much chocolate is toxic to a dog?

It depends on the theobromine load, not the weight of the chocolate. Clinical signs usually start around 20 mg of theobromine per kilogram of body weight, cardiac effects around 40 mg/kg and seizures above 60 mg/kg. In practice that is roughly 129 g of milk chocolate for a 10 kg dog, or 14.5 g of baking chocolate.

How long after eating chocolate will a dog show symptoms?

Usually within 6 to 12 hours, and signs can persist for up to 72 hours because the half-life of theobromine in dogs is about 17.5 hours. A dog that is still normal at four hours is not out of the window — the peak often comes later.

Can one square of dark chocolate hurt a dog?

It can, in a small dog. A square of 85% chocolate is about 10 g, which carries roughly 140 mg of theobromine — around 28 mg/kg for a 5 kg dog, already past the level where vomiting is expected. The same square in a 30 kg dog is under 5 mg/kg.

Should I make my dog vomit after eating chocolate?

Not unless a vet tells you to. Vomiting is only useful inside roughly two hours, it carries its own risks, and it is the wrong move for a flat-faced breed or a dog that is already drowsy or twitching. Phone first and let the vet decide.
